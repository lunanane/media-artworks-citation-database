# The Thinking Ocean

**Memo Akten, Katie Hofstadter**, 2026 · Online / net art / AI / generative / Sound

The Thinking Ocean is an online interactive generative work commissioned by the Whitney Museum for its artport website, part of the artists' series Cosmosapience. Viewers move through a simulated underwater scene of particles and bubbles that turns into patterns resembling cells, circuitry and code, driven by motion-captured choreography, with a 20-minute audio loop of spoken-word poetry; touching the screen moves the view, and the webcam can bring the viewer's surroundings into the image. It runs in the browser (threejs, WebGPU), every open instance shares a synchronised palette and atmosphere, and it is also shown as an installation and a live performance.

## In their words

> The Thinking Ocean is a provocation to consider who and what we give personhood to, and why. It questions why we rush to attribute personhood, agency and even consciousness to machines that mimic human behaviour, while overlooking the complex computations already happening in water, air and other planetary systems.
>
> — Memo Akten, Katie Hofstadter, [Project page, memo.tv](https://www.memo.tv/projects/2026/thinkingocean/)

<sub>says</sub>

> Our biology was written in water. We crawled onto land, but we brought the ocean with us. We carry it inside us. What happens when we sever this connection? When we diminish these relationships, are we diminishing our own consciousness?
>
> — Memo Akten, Katie Hofstadter, [Project page, memo.tv](https://www.memo.tv/projects/2026/thinkingocean/)

<sub>asks</sub>

> If computation is being taken as a criterion for ‘thinking,‘ or even ‘consciousness,’ then what natural systems might already qualify that we are overlooking? Why do we grant personhood to AI while denying it to natural systems like rivers, oceans, the atmosphere, and more?
>
> — Memo Akten, Katie Hofstadter, [Project page, memo.tv](https://www.memo.tv/projects/2026/thinkingocean/)

<sub>asks</sub>

> Ultimately, this work is a provocation to consider whether natural systems — that are now known to be Turing Complete, such as water and air — might be capable of thought, and if so, how are we altering those systems, those thoughts?
>
> — Memo Akten, Katie Hofstadter, [Project page, memo.tv](https://www.memo.tv/projects/2026/thinkingocean/)

# frequencies (a)

**Nicolas Bernier**, 2013 · Sound / Light / Performance / body

frequencies (a) is a sound and light performance in which tuning forks mounted in small acrylic structures on a lighting table are struck by computer-controlled solenoids, each fork linked to a contact microphone and a light. Bernier sequences the strikes, digital sounds and light pulses with Ableton Live and Max for Live, and the audience sits close to the table as the setup alternates between darkness and white light. It is the first part of Bernier's frequencies series and received the 2013 Prix Ars Electronica Golden Nica for Digital Musics & Sound Art.

## In their words

> The use of the tuning fork made perfect sense because it was a scientific precision tool in the past, and is a symbol of tonal music, but foremost because it produces a sound that is close to the pure sinewave, the very primitive sound used in electronic music composition.
>
> — Nicolas Bernier, ["Nicolas Bernier: Intimate Frequencies", Ableton blog, 28 October 2013](https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/nicolas-bernier-intimate-frequencies/)

<sub>says</sub>

> Because the piece is dealing with small objects (the tuning forks, small solenoids, gentle sounds, precision), the public needs to be close to the piece. It is built on the principle of intimacy rather than grand spectacularity.
>
> — Nicolas Bernier, ["Nicolas Bernier: Intimate Frequencies", Ableton blog, 28 October 2013](https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/nicolas-bernier-intimate-frequencies/)

<sub>says</sub>

> I actually think there is no distinction between the natural or the virtual/artificial. Everything is connected and communicating together, everything is processed in a way or another.
>
> — Nicolas Bernier, ["Nicolas Bernier: Intimate Frequencies", Ableton blog, 28 October 2013](https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/nicolas-bernier-intimate-frequencies/)

<sub>says</sub>

> frequencies is actually a series of works mainly working around the pure/impure dichotomy, declining in 3 parts: acoustic, digital and electronic.
>
> — Nicolas Bernier, [Donata Marletta, "Nicolas Bernier: between frequencies and dichotomies", Digicult, 21 May 2013](http://www.digicult.it/news/ars-electronica-prize-winners-nicolas-bernier-between-frequencies-and-dichotomies/)

<sub>says</sub>

> The (a) in the first part stands for "audio" or "acoustic" because the tuning forks are producing an almost pure acoustic tone.
>
> — Nicolas Bernier, [Donata Marletta, "Nicolas Bernier: between frequencies and dichotomies", Digicult, 21 May 2013](http://www.digicult.it/news/ars-electronica-prize-winners-nicolas-bernier-between-frequencies-and-dichotomies/)

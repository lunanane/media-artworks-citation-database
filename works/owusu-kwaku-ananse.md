# Kwaku Ananse

**Akosua Adoma Owusu**, 2013 · Screen / monitor

Kwaku Ananse is a short film (about 25 minutes; Ghana, Mexico, USA) that combines semi-autobiographical elements with the West African tale of Kwaku Ananse, a trickster who appears as both spider and man. Nyan, a young Ghanaian-American woman, travels to Ghana for the funeral of her estranged father, who had a second family there, and after the funeral goes into the forest in search of his spirit. It premiered in Berlinale Shorts at the 2013 Berlin International Film Festival.

## In their words

> I wanted to find a creative way of visually communicating an oral tale in cinematic language.
>
> — Akosua Adoma Owusu, [Rissa Papillion, 'Interview: Akosua Adoma Owusu's Kwaku Ananse at the Institute of Contemporary Art (Philadelphia)', IndieWire, 10 June 2015](https://www.indiewire.com/2015/06/interview-akosua-adoma-owusus-kwaku-ananse-at-the-institute-of-contemporary-art-philadelphia-153842/)

<sub>says</sub>

> Kwaku Ananse is semi-autobiographical. The film is about loss and forgiveness. I was making this film to let go of a lot of frustration I had built up around my father’s death, and making this film was a way for me to make peace with that grief.
>
> — Akosua Adoma Owusu, [Rissa Papillion, 'Interview: Akosua Adoma Owusu's Kwaku Ananse at the Institute of Contemporary Art (Philadelphia)', IndieWire, 10 June 2015](https://www.indiewire.com/2015/06/interview-akosua-adoma-owusus-kwaku-ananse-at-the-institute-of-contemporary-art-philadelphia-153842/)

<sub>says</sub>

> Death is a significant state of transition among the Akan of Ghana. Funerals effectively function as theater where the transition occurs.
>
> — Akosua Adoma Owusu, [Rissa Papillion, 'Interview: Akosua Adoma Owusu's Kwaku Ananse at the Institute of Contemporary Art (Philadelphia)', IndieWire, 10 June 2015](https://www.indiewire.com/2015/06/interview-akosua-adoma-owusus-kwaku-ananse-at-the-institute-of-contemporary-art-philadelphia-153842/)

<sub>says</sub>

> Anansi stories started with Ghanaians in the Ashanti culture and made it to the diaspora and I noticed that our legacy of Ananse storytelling is dying.
>
> — Akosua Adoma Owusu, [Rissa Papillion, 'Interview: Akosua Adoma Owusu's Kwaku Ananse at the Institute of Contemporary Art (Philadelphia)', IndieWire, 10 June 2015](https://www.indiewire.com/2015/06/interview-akosua-adoma-owusus-kwaku-ananse-at-the-institute-of-contemporary-art-philadelphia-153842/)

<sub>says</sub>

> Ananse teaches us that there are two sides to everything and everyone.
>
> — Akosua Adoma Owusu, [Project description by the filmmaker, quoted in Todd Brown, 'African Folk Tales Come To Life In KWAKU ANANSE', ScreenAnarchy, 5 December 2012](https://screenanarchy.com/2012/12/african-folk-tales-come-to-life-in-kwaku-ananse.html)

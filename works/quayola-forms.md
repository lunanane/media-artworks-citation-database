# Forms

**Quayola, Memo Akten**, 2013 · Projection / Screen / monitor / Camera / computer vision

A video work and installation by Quayola and Memo Akten made from archive footage of athletes (swimmers, divers, pole vaulters, gymnasts), whose movements are tracked and used to drive physics-based simulations that become abstract moving sculptures. In the National Media Museum installation (Bradford, 2012) the animation was projected on a large screen, with a smaller screen showing the source footage and the individual layers. It received the Golden Nica for Computer Animation / Film / VFX at Prix Ars Electronica 2013.

## In their words

> Rather than focusing on observable trajectories, we chose to explore techniques of extrapolation to sculpt abstract forms, visualizing unseen relationships—power, balance, grace, and conflict—between the body and its surroundings.
>
> — Quayola and Memo Akten, ['Quayola And Memo Akten Translate Athletic Movements Into Abstract Animations', The Creators Project / VICE, 5 March 2012](https://www.vice.com/en/article/quayola-and-memo-akten-translate-athletic-movements-into-abstract-animations/)

> We wanted to deconstruct these disciplines, and interrogate them from an exclusively mechanical and aesthetic point of view—concentrating on the invisible forces generated by and influencing the movement.
>
> — Quayola and Memo Akten, ['Quayola And Memo Akten Translate Athletic Movements Into Abstract Animations', The Creators Project / VICE, 5 March 2012](https://www.vice.com/en/article/quayola-and-memo-akten-translate-athletic-movements-into-abstract-animations/)

<sub>says</sub>

> In this project we shifted the attention from the athlete’s aim of winning, to the actual mechanics of his body. We are fascinated by the observation and study of the invisible.
>
> — Quayola and Memo Akten, [Pasquale Napolitano, 'Quayola & Memo Akten: "Forms". Something about Life, at Ars Electronica Prix 2013', Digicult, 28 May 2013](https://digicult.it/news/quayola-and-memo-atken-forms-something-about-life-at-ars-electronica-prix-2013/)

<sub>says</sub>

> We are not straight data visualizers. Yes we start with data, but instead of showing the data untouched, raw, straight up; we add an artistic interpretation to it, we embellish it, we accentuate it, we look for hidden relationships and qualities and amplify them.
>
> — Quayola and Memo Akten, [Pasquale Napolitano, 'Quayola & Memo Akten: "Forms". Something about Life, at Ars Electronica Prix 2013', Digicult, 28 May 2013](https://digicult.it/news/quayola-and-memo-atken-forms-something-about-life-at-ars-electronica-prix-2013/)

>
> — Memo Akten, [Forms (2011), work page on memo.tv (artist's site)](https://www.memo.tv/works/forms/)

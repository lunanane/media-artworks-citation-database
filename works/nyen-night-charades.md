# Night Charades

**Ho Tzu Nyen**, 2025 · Screen / monitor / AI / generative

Night Charades is a silent AI-generated animation commissioned for the LED facade of the M+ museum in Hong Kong, shown nightly from 22 March to 29 June 2025. Animated characters mime around 50 scenes, gestures and poses from 1980s and 1990s Hong Kong cinema, re-enacting roles originally played by actors such as Leslie Cheung, Maggie Cheung and Chow Yun-fat. An algorithm continuously reshuffles the scenes and characters into new combinations, viewed by passers-by across Victoria Harbour.

## In their words

> The silence of this transmission—the absence of a soundtrack—led me to the idea of charade, which is a game as well as a performance in the warmth of a home. I am intrigued by the notion of transforming a game that is usually played within the confines of a home with friends and family into something not only extremely public but also distantly familiar yet alien.
>
> — Ho Tzu Nyen, [M+ Magazine, 'Night Charades: Reimagining Hong Kong Cinema' (interview by Ariadne Long and Silke Schmickl)](https://mplus.org.hk/en/magazine/night-charades-reimaging-hong-kong-cinema)

> The one question I asked and continue to ask myself is: ‘Who is this work for?’
>
> — Ho Tzu Nyen, [M+ Magazine, 'Night Charades: Reimagining Hong Kong Cinema' (interview by Ariadne Long and Silke Schmickl)](https://mplus.org.hk/en/magazine/night-charades-reimaging-hong-kong-cinema)

<sub>asks</sub>

> But I’ve become increasingly troubled by the cruelty of decision that lies in the selection process of filmmaking—the selection of that one perfect angle, that one perfect moment, and that one perfect shot, to be placed in the one perfect sequence to obtain that perfect timeline. The final result is that one perfect film by the one singular auteur. However, what interests me, at this moment, is no longer this search for the ‘one’, but rather how we can create a system that can continuously generate multiple versions of itself, and how this multiplicity can be shaped, as form.
>
> — Ho Tzu Nyen, [M+ Magazine, 'Night Charades: Reimagining Hong Kong Cinema' (interview by Ariadne Long and Silke Schmickl)](https://mplus.org.hk/en/magazine/night-charades-reimaging-hong-kong-cinema)

<sub>says</sub>

> I was struck by how much of Hong Kong cinema and Cantopop were described as having their ‘Golden Ages’ in the 1980s and 1990s. Of course, I can understand why these are labelled as such, but this way of attributing an unsurpassable peak to past achievements nevertheless troubled me.
>
> — Ho Tzu Nyen, [M+ Magazine, 'Night Charades: Reimagining Hong Kong Cinema' (interview by Ariadne Long and Silke Schmickl)](https://mplus.org.hk/en/magazine/night-charades-reimaging-hong-kong-cinema)

<sub>says</sub>

> What I resist is the single version, the single timeline. AI is a way for me to work with multiple versions, which is to say that there is no perfect version, it’s always slightly imperfect, like a charade.
>
> — Ho Tzu Nyen, [The Art Newspaper, 'Ho Tzu Nyen on how AI leads to the cinematic past' (interview)](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/03/28/ho-tzu-nyen-on-how-ai-leads-to-the-cinematic-past)

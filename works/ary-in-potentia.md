# In Potentia

**Guy Ben-Ary, Kirsten Hudson**, 2012 · Material / sculpture / Sound / Sensor / interaction

In Potentia is an installation in which human foreskin cells are reprogrammed with the induced pluripotent stem cell (iPS) technique into stem cells and then into neurons, forming a living neural network kept in a purpose-built sculptural incubator with an automated feeding and waste system. A multi-electrode array records the network's electrical activity and converts it into sound, so visitors listen to the neural culture rather than see it, as the cells are not visible to the naked eye. It was made with Stuart Hodgetts and Mark Lawson and shown at Kibla, Maribor (2012) and at ISEA2013, Powerhouse Museum, Sydney, among other venues.

## In their words

> Interested in how art has the potential to problematise the shifting forces that govern and determine life, death and personhood, we developed in potēntia: a speculative techno-scientific experiment with disembodied human material, diagnostic equipment and a stem cell reprogramming technique called iPS.
>
> — Guy Ben-Ary and Kirsten Hudson, [In Potentia, guybenary.com](https://www.guybenary.com/projects/in-potentia.html)

<sub>says · unsigned project text</sub>

> We ask: as it is now possible to bio-engineer a neural network or biological brain, what potential do we now have to bio-engineer conscious, sentient beings and where exactly would these liminal lives fit within our problematic anthropocentric species hierarchy?
>
> — Guy Ben-Ary and Kirsten Hudson, [In Potentia, guybenary.com](https://www.guybenary.com/projects/in-potentia.html)

<sub>asks · unsigned project text</sub>

> By creating an object that although appears to contain an empty Petri dish (as the cells are unperceivable to the naked eye) but in fact contains a “live” and active neural network, with in potēntia we ask viewers to question the means with which they judge and determine truth and reality, and in turn what that might mean in terms of how we currently determine life, death and personhood in the 21st century.
>
> — Guy Ben-Ary and Kirsten Hudson, [In Potentia, guybenary.com](https://www.guybenary.com/projects/in-potentia.html)

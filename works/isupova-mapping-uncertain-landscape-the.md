# Mapping Uncertain Landscape: The Satellite

**Sofia Isupova**, 2024 · Robotics / kinetics / Network / data / Text / print

An installation in which a plotter machine built by the artist continuously draws maps of Ukrainian terrain on a large roll of paper, translating vegetation-index data (NDRE/NDVI) from the commercial crop-monitoring satellite EOS SAT into colours. The first version focuses on a road between Ivankiv and the Belarus border; later map series, made with ecologists and earth scientists, follow vegetation changes around the destruction of the Kakhovka Dam (6 June 2023) and the first Russian offensive on Kyiv. It was a diploma project at HEAD Geneva and received an Honorary Mention at the S+T+ARTS Prize 2024.

## In their words

> When I went to Kyiv in August of 2022, my eyes were peeled to the landscape. I needed the see, what has changed.
>
> — Sonya (Sofia) Isupova, [Project page, artist website](https://sonyaisupova.cargo.site/The-Satelitte)

<sub>says</sub>

> In order to do that I built a map-making machine, a forever watchful eye: focusing on a specific location: this road between Ivankiv and the Belarus border, 50 km from Chornobyl’s Zone of Exclusion. 90km to Kyiv.
>
> — Sonya (Sofia) Isupova, [Project page, artist website](https://sonyaisupova.cargo.site/The-Satelitte)

<sub>says</sub>

> I started with building a machine, a metaphorical satellite, hovering above the land, mapping the data with a limited amount of accuracy, transforming satellite images into maps of the territory. I became interested in the relationship between humans and the machine.
>
> — Sonya (Sofia) Isupova, [Project page, artist website](https://sonyaisupova.cargo.site/The-Satelitte)

<sub>says</sub>

> Mapping Uncertain Landscape: The Satellite serves as a means for us to bear witness—an arduous task carried out by Ukrainians on a daily basis. The notion that something traumatic requires witnessing, seeing, and hearing forms a cornerstone of journalism. This responsibility has now extended to artists, designers, and cultural workers in Ukraine. Maps and mapping serve as a means for us, as artists and designers, to continue this work of bearing witness to the brutality of war, even if unsuccessfully.
>
> — Sofia Isupova (project text), [Project text, Prix Ars Electronica / S+T+ARTS Prize 2024 catalogue](https://ars.electronica.art/hope/files/2024/08/prix2024.pdf)

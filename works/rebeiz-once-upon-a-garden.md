# Once Upon a Garden

**Linda Dounia Rebeiz**, 2022 · AI / generative / Screen / monitor

An ongoing series (begun 2021, first shown as a special project commissioned by ART X Lagos in 2022) in which the artist trained a GAN, and later diffusion and video models, on records of extinct and critically endangered flora from the Sahel region of West Africa to generate images of these plants. In the first chapter the generated flowers were curated, animated and composited into artificial gardens installed at exhibitions; the complete series (2022–2024) presents 50 speculative flora across five chapters.

## In their words

> Once Upon A Garden became a dystopian projection of a likely outcome of global warming where all plants and flowers from the West Africa region have disappeared from Earth. It depicted a reality where humans have to live with simulated images of these plants and flowers, based on a patchy digital memory of them.
>
> — Linda Dounia Rebeiz, [It's Nice That, essay by the artist, 1 November 2023](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/linda-dounia-rebeiz-ai-bias-technology-creative-industry-art-011123)

<sub>says</sub>

> This body of work also became an allegory for the lossy reflection of our humanity by AI. What we don’t include in training today contributes to a much hazier and incomplete understanding of our world.
>
> — Linda Dounia Rebeiz, [It's Nice That, essay by the artist, 1 November 2023](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/linda-dounia-rebeiz-ai-bias-technology-creative-industry-art-011123)

<sub>says</sub>

> The series also exposes just how poorly we are recording the extensive loss of wildlife, especially in certain parts of the world. This brings us back to a more general issue we have with the data we use to train AI—it’s missing or overlooking a great deal.
>
> — Linda Dounia, [AI Art Questionnaire (Anika Meier), interview](https://anikameierstatusupdate.substack.com/p/ai-art-questionnaire-linda-dounia)

>
> — Linda Dounia, [Le Random, interview with Peter Bauman, 19 February 2024](https://www.lerandom.art/editorial/linda-dounia-on-memory-machines)

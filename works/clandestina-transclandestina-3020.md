# TRANSCLANDESTINA 3020

**Manauara Clandestina**, 2025 · Performance / body / Material / sculpture / Screen / monitor

A multidisciplinary work commissioned for the 36th Bienal de São Paulo, made with collaborators from fashion, music and film: a clothing line made from transformed and repurposed worker's uniforms, a fashion show at the Bienal's opening, a sculptural installation of the garments, and a film documenting the creative process. In the installation, visitors meet garments on wooden structures and scaffolding, Polaroid and digital photographs of trans people, and a TV showing the film.

## In their words

> We have realised several large performances together so far, including: Travesti Viva (2016) – asserting and celebrating the travesti community’s existence and right to existence, and our very being alive against the odds; Transclandestina (2019) – discussing the migration processes of northern trans people to southern Brazil and their consequent marginalisation; and most recently Brazil: The Travesti World Champion (2019) – in which we reclaimed the use of the colours of the Brazilian flag, which had been appropriated by the extreme right.
>
> — Manauara Clandestina, [In Conversation: Manauara Clandestina and Victoria Sin, Delfina Foundation (2020)](https://www.delfinafoundation.com/platform/in-conversation-manauara-clandestina-and-victoria-sin/)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> Since coming to London – especially when I just arrived and the restrictions due to COVID-19 were a bit lighter – I started doing research with practical clothing, those used by construction workers and people working on the streets. Together with fashion designer Murilo Tadashi we started upcycling cheap high-vis clothes and I am working on a video outcome from this.
>
> — Manauara Clandestina, [In Conversation: Manauara Clandestina and Victoria Sin, Delfina Foundation (2020)](https://www.delfinafoundation.com/platform/in-conversation-manauara-clandestina-and-victoria-sin/)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> Yes, the base of our oppression is this system of cisgender hetero-normativity, which washes out transgender lives and everything else that could question or challenge it. Our trans community is in never ending fight, in fact it’s more like a war than a battle, given it’s on so many fronts. For example, that of self-representation. The fight to represent for ourselves our lives in media – like on TV and in cinema.
>
> — Manauara Clandestina, [In Conversation: Manauara Clandestina and Victoria Sin, Delfina Foundation (2020)](https://www.delfinafoundation.com/platform/in-conversation-manauara-clandestina-and-victoria-sin/)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> Transvestites have everything to do with fashion and cinema. I want to make more films and see more self-respecting transvestites in their roles; not only as actresses, but also as directors and photographers who create our imaginary of Brazil and our daily lives,
>
> — Manauara Clandestina, [MASP, Video Room: Manauara Clandestina (exhibition page)](https://www.masp.org.br/en/exhibitions/video-room-manauara-clandestina)

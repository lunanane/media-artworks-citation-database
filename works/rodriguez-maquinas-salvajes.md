# Máquinas Salvajes

**Jonathan Torres Rodríguez**, 2023 · Robotics / kinetics / Material / sculpture / Sensor / interaction

Máquinas Salvajes (Wild Machines) consists of biodegradable mechanical sculptures with wooden skeletons and biopolymer skins (agar-agar and gelatin) made from materials of Costa Rican ecosystems. Two were left in the Sarapiquí rainforest and in Orotina to decay or be eaten, which was documented on video; in the exhibition (Lentos Museum, Linz, 2023) the videos are shown with a third sculpture that a sensor sprays with water when visitors are present, gradually degrading it. The project received a 2023 CIFO-Ars Electronica Award.

## In their words

> It allows me to explore a universe in which these robotic beings exist and, through them, to scrutinize ideas around possible technologies that are distanced from the needs of the current economic system of extraction and consumption, and are much closer to the needs of ecosystems.
>
> — Jonathan Torres Rodríguez, [Interview, Ding Magazine (Superrr), issue 5](https://dingdingding.org/issue-5/1-interview-with-jonathan-torres-rodriguez/)

<sub>says</sub>

> The idea is to make visible a series of biodegradable, machinic beings, to materialize fragments of their still non-existent form, and be able to perceive some of their characteristics – at least those that allow us to think about their possible existence and the way they exist, the way they die, and the way they reintegrate – and so move them away from the current system of extraction and linear use of resources, and bring them closer to the systems of what is alive.
>
> — Jonathan Torres Rodríguez, [Interview, Ding Magazine (Superrr), issue 5](https://dingdingding.org/issue-5/1-interview-with-jonathan-torres-rodriguez/)

<sub>says</sub>

> Thus, these works, these artifacts, by emulating the way biological systems die, allow us to think of them as alive. I consider that in this act is an enormous potency and it leads me to profound conceptual questions: how do machines die, or in what way should technologies die? What poetics are hidden in the death of a machinic being?
>
> — Jonathan Torres Rodríguez, [Interview, Ding Magazine (Superrr), issue 5](https://dingdingding.org/issue-5/1-interview-with-jonathan-torres-rodriguez/)

<sub>asks</sub>

> If technological devices generate discourse and create knowledge (Deleuze), it is urgent to think of the nature of these technologies. What if machines could become compost? Compost that decomposes in the modern world and blends in with the leaves and bugs. How could we achieve it? What new processes would we need? How can we all conceive of such a thing?
>
> — Jonathan Torres Rodríguez, [Project text, Ars Electronica Festival 2023](https://ars.electronica.art/who-owns-the-truth/?p=1804)

<sub>asks · unsigned project text</sub>

> Whenever we go somewhere, what we do is take things. We hardly ever stop to think about these ways we have, as a species, of relating to the rest of the world.
>
> — Jonathan Torres Rodríguez, [La Nación (Costa Rica), Revista Dominical (translated from Spanish)](https://www.nacion.com/revista-dominical/el-hombre-que-fabrica-insectos-conozca-el-trabajo/QKBTIQRHBNBKXEN4NSZRC3RB7Y/story/)

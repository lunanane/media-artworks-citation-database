# Hand-Me-Downs

**Yto Barrada**, 2011 · Screen / monitor / Sound

Hand-Me-Downs is a film in which Yto Barrada narrates sixteen family 'myths' over strangers' home movies and archival films from the last half-century in Morocco. According to the Walker Art Center, the footage is Super 8 home movies found at Moroccan flea markets; Barrada says the material came to her through a French archive of amateur and propaganda films. It was made for RIFFS, her Deutsche Bank Artist of the Year 2011 exhibition, which travelled to Ikon Gallery, Birmingham, in 2012.

## In their words

> The idea was never to check. My mother and my aunt, when viewing the film, didn’t even pay any attention to the fact that the images were not theirs.
>
> — Yto Barrada, [Ian Francis, 'Tangerine dreams: Yto Barrada', Sight and Sound (BFI), interview, 2012](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/tangerine-dreams-yto-barrada)

> Now I’ve got a whole series of family corrections, and I think I’ll do a little publication with every story corrected.
>
> — Yto Barrada, [Ian Francis, 'Tangerine dreams: Yto Barrada', Sight and Sound (BFI), interview, 2012](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/tangerine-dreams-yto-barrada)

> Hand-Me-Downs is a good example of how my work and the Cinémathèque meet. We had a letter from a French archive which collects amateur and propaganda films, and when I looked at these home movies I just felt I had to do something with them.
>
> — Yto Barrada, [Ian Francis, 'Tangerine dreams: Yto Barrada', Sight and Sound (BFI), interview, 2012](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/tangerine-dreams-yto-barrada)

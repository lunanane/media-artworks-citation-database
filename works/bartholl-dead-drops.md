# Dead Drops

**Aram Bartholl**, 2010 · Network / data / Material / sculpture

Dead Drops is an anonymous, offline, peer-to-peer file-sharing network in public space: USB flash drives are cemented into walls, buildings and curbs, each installed empty except for a readme.txt file. Anyone can plug a laptop into a drop to leave or copy files, and anyone may install a new drop and submit its location to the project's database. Bartholl installed the first five in New York in October 2010 during an Eyebeam residency; the project was shown in MoMA's Talk to Me (2011) and has since spread to many countries.

## In their words

> Dead drops started, for me, with this picture of your flash drive in the wall, and just the gesture of — you walk up with your laptop and you connect it to the building, to the city. The files are literally cemented into the building, instead of all this server-based connection.
>
> — Aram Bartholl, [Jillian Steinhauer, 'Somewhere Between Cyber and Real: An Interview with Aram Bartholl', Hyperallergic, 20 August 2012](https://hyperallergic.com/somewhere-between-cyber-and-real-an-interview-with-aram-bartholl/)

<sub>says</sub>

> And it made so much sense with the whole idea of having it in public, accessible to everyone, and the internet censorship discussion.
>
> — Aram Bartholl, [Jillian Steinhauer, 'Somewhere Between Cyber and Real: An Interview with Aram Bartholl', Hyperallergic, 20 August 2012](https://hyperallergic.com/somewhere-between-cyber-and-real-an-interview-with-aram-bartholl/)

<sub>says</sub>

> A Dead Drop is a naked piece of passively powered Universal Serial Bus technology embedded into the city, the only true public space. In an era of growing clouds and fancy new devices without access to local files we need to rethink the freedom and distribution of data.
>
> — Aram Bartholl, [The Dead Drops Manifesto, deaddrops.com (signed Aram Bartholl 2010)](https://deaddrops.com/manifesto/)

<sub>says</sub>

> Your knees on the ground or a dirty jacket on the wall is what it takes share files offline.
>
> — Aram Bartholl, [The Dead Drops Manifesto, deaddrops.com (signed Aram Bartholl 2010)](https://deaddrops.com/manifesto/)

<sub>says</sub>

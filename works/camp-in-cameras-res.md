# In Cameras Res

**CAMP**, 2019 · Screen / monitor / Camera / computer vision / Network / data

In Cameras Res is a three-channel moving-image installation by CAMP, commissioned by De Appel, Amsterdam, for which automated CCTV (pan-tilt-zoom) cameras were programmed to film landscape sequences of the city. Each film was shot from a single camera position on a historical building (the former Royal Dutch Shell headquarters, now A'DAM Tower; the former NHM building, now Stadsarchief Amsterdam; the former Tetterode printing foundry), each with a distinct editing technique, and certain people recur in the frame. It was shown at De Appel from 16 March to 20 April 2019.

## In their words

> Here, CAMP speculates about future images and future awareness, via the question: What can cinema be in this time where there are more cameras than people?
>
> — CAMP (studio website), [In Cameras Res, 2019, work page, studio.camp](https://studio.camp/works/incameras)

<sub>asks · unsigned project text</sub>

> How can we reconnect the vastness and inhumanity of CCTVs technological side to a perceptual system called cinema? This question is also related to others. In Amsterdam, a city with a rich tradition of new media theory and art, a poster at the Waag put it bluntly: ‘Smart city - Dumb citizens’. Of course this is a provocation, but it is also the type of local challenge In Cameras Res enters into.
>
> — CAMP, [Interview with CAMP reproduced on the In Cameras Res work page, studio.camp (exhibition handout, De Appel)](https://studio.camp/works/incameras)

<sub>asks</sub>

> The point in both instances is to introduce some doubt as to who is an actor and who isn’t. This creates a tension around awareness that is something like an uncanny reverse of fiction cinema.
>
> — CAMP, [Interview with CAMP reproduced on the In Cameras Res work page, studio.camp](https://studio.camp/works/incameras)

<sub>says</sub>

> After all, the whole of CCTV is a kind of monumental ecosystem of image waste, waiting for an event.
>
> — CAMP, [Interview with CAMP reproduced on the In Cameras Res work page, studio.camp](https://studio.camp/works/incameras)

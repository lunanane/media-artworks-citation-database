# PL'AI

**Špela Petrič**, 2021 · Robotics / kinetics / AI / generative / Camera / computer vision

An installation lasting several months in which cucumber plants grown from seed and an AI-controlled robot about three metres tall interact. The robot moves 36 bouncy balls on stainless steel threads above a tray of plants; a camera and a 3D scanner (scanning every 15 minutes) feed the AI, and when a plant's tendril grabs a ball, that ball stops moving. Produced in 2020 by Kersnikova Institute / Kapelica Gallery (European ARTificial Intelligence Lab), it received an Honorary Mention at Prix Ars Electronica 2021.

## In their words

> The point of departure was the question: what would it mean to make an artificial intelligence (AI) that thinks of itself as a plant? This idea is derived from my curiosity about the wider relationship between people and plants as well as the increasing use of machine learning algorithms that inform our understanding of the world.
>
> — Špela Petrič, ["PL'AI: where plant and machine playfully meet", Waag](https://waag.org/en/article/plai-where-plant-and-machine-playfully-meet)

<sub>asks</sub>

> I wanted to reframe our relationship with plants through play, by creating an alternative for the current hierarchical, rational way of thinking that eventually led to a disenchantment of the world. Play is an activity that you do for itself, as something pleasurable. With plants you don’t see emotions like play and pleasure, but does that mean that it doesn’t exist?
>
> — Špela Petrič, ["PL'AI: where plant and machine playfully meet", Waag](https://waag.org/en/article/plai-where-plant-and-machine-playfully-meet)

<sub>asks</sub>

> It all got me thinking: What is this humanness that we’re losing? I realized that this kind of humanness, this kind of consideration, is what has also been lacking in our relationship to plants.
>
> — Špela Petrič, ["In the Algorithm’s Eyes, 'We Are Already Plants'", Getty News (Q&A)](https://www.getty.edu/news/bio-artist-spela-petric-interview-q-and-a)

<sub>asks</sub>

> If I were a plant, I would like an AI robot to be concerned with giving me joy. I don’t want it to improve me, extract from me, or use me to do something that is good for someone else—just play with me.
>
> — Špela Petrič, ["In the Algorithm’s Eyes, 'We Are Already Plants'", Getty News (Q&A)](https://www.getty.edu/news/bio-artist-spela-petric-interview-q-and-a)

<sub>says</sub>

> PL’AI, the third work from Špela Petrič’s opus PLANT-MACHINE, embraces the notion of a play as an ontological condition of all living bodies, including plants. The act of playing, unlike games, which are limited by clear rules or goals, reflects the curiosity of existence and is therefore at the heart of (self)knowing.
>
> — Špela Petrič (project text), [PL'AI — project page, spelapetric.org](https://www.spelapetric.org/plai/)

# Nairobi Berries

**Ng'endo Mukii**, 2017 · VR / AR / XR / Sound

A 360-degree virtual reality film for mobile VR that combines live-action footage of Nairobi with animation and a lyrical voice-over by Mukii; its synopsis describes two women and a man who wrangle in forests full of smoke and beneath still waters. The viewer, wearing a headset, is moved through city locations with vertical camera movements, into the ground and up from under water, ending at night under water. It was made with Electric South's New Dimensions grant and won the first Immersive Encounters Grand Prix at Encounters Film Festival in 2017.

## In their words

> When I was story-boarding the film, my main concern was to use the space to create a dream-like state and to make the audience occupy the space with the feeling that this created world is constantly happening around them. I wanted it to feel like a waking dream.
>
> — Ng'endo Mukii, ['Award Winner Interview: Ng'endo Mukii', Encounters Film Festival, words Isabella Coombes (archived)](http://web.archive.org/web/20180606185224/http://encounters-festival.org.uk:80/immersive-encounters-award-winner-interview-ngendo-mukii/)

> — Ng'endo Mukii, ['Award Winner Interview: Ng'endo Mukii', Encounters Film Festival (archived)](http://web.archive.org/web/20180606185224/http://encounters-festival.org.uk:80/immersive-encounters-award-winner-interview-ngendo-mukii/)

<sub>says</sub>

> The ebb and flow of the city affects how and when we interact, we learn to socially slip around each other, and through backstreets to avoid traffic. We’re always twisting and turning. Culturally and creatively, I feel we do these things as well. Specifically in Nairobi Berries, I think the poetry and imagery I created reflect this.
>
> — Ng'endo Mukii, ['Award Winner Interview: Ng'endo Mukii', Encounters Film Festival (archived)](http://web.archive.org/web/20180606185224/http://encounters-festival.org.uk:80/immersive-encounters-award-winner-interview-ngendo-mukii/)

<sub>says</sub>

> The title, Nairobi Berries, is a play on words for us. We often talk of ‘Nairoberry’ which refers to our high rate of crimes and robbery. I was contemplating on the why of this culture, what we are seeking that creates pervasive hording and petty crime. These are the ‘fruits promised.’
>
> — Ng'endo Mukii, [MIT Docubase, 'Nairobi Berries' project page (quoted statement, credited 'Ng’endo Mukii, Author')](https://docubase.mit.edu/project/nairobi-berries)

<sub>says</sub>

> My Nairobi is an ode to my city, from a quietly visceral perspective. It uses the physical environments explored in VR to delve into the spirit of the people who inhabit the boundaries of its social walls.
>
> — Ng'endo Mukii (work statement), [Radiance VR, artist page 'Ngendo Mukii' – Nairobi Berries description (unsigned, first person)](https://www.radiancevr.co/artists/ngendo-mukii/)

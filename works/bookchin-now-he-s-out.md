# Now he's out in public and everyone can see

**Natalie Bookchin**, 2012 · Screen / monitor / Online / net art / Sound

Now he's out in public and everyone can see is an 18-channel video installation in which 18 monitors of different sizes hang on cables at varying heights around a darkened room. A 16-minute narrative edits together excerpts from hundreds of YouTube vlogs in which people comment on viral media scandals involving four famous African American men, whose names are removed so that the speakers seem to discuss one unnamed man; voices sometimes speak in unison and sometimes answer or contradict each other. Visitors move around the room to follow speakers who are never all in view at once. It premiered at LACE (Los Angeles Contemporary Exhibitions) in 2012 and was remade as a single-channel film in 2017.

## In their words

> is an 18-channel video installation on collective performances of race and racial anxiety in the Obama era. A 16-minute narrative samples from hundreds of online vlogs that narrate and editorialize on a series of viral media scandals involving famous African American men.
>
> — Natalie Bookchin (artist's website), [Now he's out in public and everyone can see, project page, bookchin.net (Wayback Machine copy, 2015)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150426010030/http://bookchin.net/projects/out-in-public.html)

<sub>says · unsigned project text</sub>

> Weaving the various scandals and rumors together and removing the names suggest that the specifics don’t really matter. The man in question is a figment of the speakers’ and the viewers’ imaginations, composed of rumours and gossip, speculations, and judgements. The language and the stories themselves keep repeating — different man, different incident, but same old story.
>
> — Natalie Bookchin, ['Natalie Bookchin in Conversation with Alexandra Juhasz: Performances of Race and White Hegemony on YouTube', in Fabricating Publics, eds. Bill Balaskas and Carolina Rito (Open Humanities Press, 2021), PDF on bookchin.net](https://bookchin.net/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Natalie-Bookchin-in-Conversation-with-Juhasz-2021.pdf)

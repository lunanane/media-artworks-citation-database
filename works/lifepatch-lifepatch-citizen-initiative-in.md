# lifepatch - citizen initiative in art, science and technology

**Lifepatch**, 2014 · Network / data

A community-based, cross-disciplinary organisation in Yogyakarta, Indonesia, formed on 26 March 2012, that runs workshops, research projects and a citizen lab in art, science and technology (e.g. fermentation, DIY electronics, webcam microscopes, Jogja River Project water sampling) and documents its activities on an open wiki. It received an Honorary Mention in the Digital Communities category of Prix Ars Electronica 2014.

## In their words

> At Lifepatch, we question global technologies and science - the trends. We realised that Indonesia lost many traditional sciences during colonisation. We try to bring this specific issue of traditional science into our practices because we think it is very important and very interesting.
>
> — Andreas Siagian (Lifepatch), ["Between science, art and social design there is community", interview by Rebecca Conroy, Inside Indonesia 118, 2014](https://www.insideindonesia.org/editions/edition-118-oct-dec-2014/between-science-art-and-social-design-there-is-community)

> Our tagline is ‘citizen initiative’ because our field is both art and science, and we really depend on the collaboration between people who are involved in both these practices. If we call it an art initiative, then scientists don’t feel they have a space in that.
>
> — Andreas Siagian (Lifepatch), ["Between science, art and social design there is community", interview by Rebecca Conroy, Inside Indonesia 118, 2014](https://www.insideindonesia.org/editions/edition-118-oct-dec-2014/between-science-art-and-social-design-there-is-community)

<sub>says</sub>

> That is why the main focus of Lifepatch is on sharing knowledge with the people.
>
> — Andreas Siagian (Lifepatch), ["Between science, art and social design there is community", interview by Rebecca Conroy, Inside Indonesia 118, 2014](https://www.insideindonesia.org/editions/edition-118-oct-dec-2014/between-science-art-and-social-design-there-is-community)

> Another reason we don't define ourselves as a hackerspace is that all of the references that point to hackerspaces basically refer to something that started in computer clubs in the United States in the 1970s. And these notions miss the essential context of Indonesian collectivity that is within our roots.
>
> — Andreas Siagian (Lifepatch), [Interview with Minoru Hatanaka, Asia Hundreds, The Japan Foundation Asia Center, 2016](https://asiawa.jpf.go.jp/en/culture/features/asiahundreds014/)

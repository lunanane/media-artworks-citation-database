# Nuit Blanche

**Arev Manoukian, Marc-André Gray (National Film Board of Canada)**, 2010 · Screen / monitor

Nuit Blanche is a black-and-white short film of about four minutes, directed by Arev Manoukian with visual effects by Marc-André Gray, set on a 1950s cobblestone street where a man looks at a woman in a café across the street. Live action shot at up to 2,000 frames per second is combined with 3-D effects and matte paintings, so the moment of attraction between the two strangers is stretched into slow motion as glass and water burst around them. It received the Golden Nica for Computer Animation / Film / VFX at Prix Ars Electronica 2010.

## In their words

> The idea for Nuit Blanche came from the notion of having a special moment with a total stranger, which happens to everyone, especially in a large metropolitan city. It lasts for a split second, then things get awkward, so we turn away. I wanted to take that moment of attraction and stretch it in a hyper‐real fantasy where things unfold like slow-moving photographs.
>
> — Arev Manoukian, [Quoted in Justice Mitchell, "Exploding Noir At 2,000 Frames Per Second" (blog post), 2010](https://justice-mitchell.squarespace.com/justice-mitchell/2010/4/12/exploding-noir-at-2000-frames-per-second.html)

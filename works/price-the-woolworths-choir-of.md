# The Woolworths Choir of 1979

**Elizabeth Price**, 2012 · Screen / monitor / Sound / Text / print

The Woolworths Choir of 1979 is a single-channel HD video installation of about 20 minutes in three episodes. The first uses archival photographs and art-historical captions to describe the choir of a Gothic church; the second edits internet-streamed recordings of singers and dancers into a single choreography of hand gestures; the third assembles news footage, witness statements, inquest testimony and reconstructions of the fatal 1979 fire at the Woolworths store in Manchester. A recurring twisting-wrist gesture and finger clicks link the parts; it was shown in the 2012 Turner Prize exhibition at Tate Britain, for which Price won the prize.

## In their words

> I wanted this story to be a minor but significant social history. I decided on the history of the fatal fire in the Woolworths department store in 1979, which has been a relatively forgotten corporate disaster.
>
> — Elizabeth Price, [Interview with Elizabeth Price, The Model, Sligo](https://themodel.ie/?p=971)

> I really wanted to extend the idea of a choir or a voice or a collective of people speaking and singing together to tell that story of the fire.

> — Elizabeth Price, [Interview with Elizabeth Price, The Model, Sligo](https://themodel.ie/?p=971)

<sub>says</sub>

> Both the architectural and musical ‘choir’, are related terms derived from the ‘chorus’ of classical Greek theatre: a circle of singers and dancers who narrate the action. And these manifold meanings underpin the structure of the work: in the sequence of the three episodes an auditorium is constructed, a chorus is assembled, and finally, a tragedy is narrated.
>
> — Elizabeth Price, [The Woolworths Choir of 1979, project text, elizabethprice.site](https://elizabethprice.site/the-woolworths-choir-of-1979)

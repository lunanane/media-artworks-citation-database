# Displuvium

**Fragmentin**, 2019 · Material / sculpture / Screen / monitor / Network / data

An installation in the form of a water-filled pool on the floor (weathering steel, black aluminium, distilled water), in which tiny underwater 3D-printed nozzles generate drops so the visitor sees 'rain' falling on the water surface. Two wall screens show a map of twelve historical rainfall events, some natural and some caused by human intervention such as cloud seeding; when controlled events are shown, the drops form recognisable patterns. Made with designer Renaud Defrancesco; shown in the 'Swiss Media Art' exhibition (!Mediengruppe Bitnik, Fragmentin, Lauren Huret) at H3K, Basel, and later in stone and circular editions.

## In their words

> Displuvium is an artistic research that examines the human desire to control our natural environment, particularly meteorological phenomena.
>
> — Fragmentin, [Collective's project text, Displuvium (weathering steel oval edition), fragment.in](https://fragment.in/artworks/displuvium/)

<sub>says</sub>

> Is the visitor able to perceive the gradual transformation of the rain’s rhythms? When does seemingly natural rainfall reveal itself as a controlled and artificially created downpour?
>
> — Fragmentin, [Collective's project text, Displuvium (weathering steel oval edition), fragment.in](https://fragment.in/artworks/displuvium/)

<sub>asks</sub>

> Displuvium aims to demonstrate the link between humans and nature. It illustrates the ease with which it is now possible to reproduce natural phenomena, thanks to technological breakthroughs. However, this installation also reveals the limits of our control over the environment: the failures which dot the sequence remind us that some things are patently beyond our control.
>
> — Fragmentin, [Collective's project text, Displuvium (weathering steel oval edition), fragment.in](https://fragment.in/artworks/displuvium/)

<sub>says</sub>

> The displuvium was thus an everyday source of distraction and information, not unlike the digital screens of today.
>
> — Fragmentin, [Collective's project text, Displuvium (weathering steel oval edition), fragment.in](https://fragment.in/artworks/displuvium/)

# Knit II

**Kawita Vatanajyankur**, 2021 · Screen / monitor / Performance / body

Knit II (2021) is a 4K video by Kawita Vatanajyankur, listed on the Simon Lee Foundation Institute of Contemporary Asian Art (AGWA) artist page. It belongs to her knitting works within the Performing Textiles series, in which the artist uses her own body as the mechanism of a knitting machine, weaving red yarn into a web-like fabric on camera. Viewers watch the recorded performance as a video.

## In their words

> Knit is the first video in the series in which I use my body to weave a spider web. During my research, I interviewed labourers in factories who said they felt they were treated like machines or objects and had to work repetitively.
>
> — Kawita Vatanajyankur, [Bangkok Post, 'Giving the voiceless a voice' (Suwitcha Chaiyong, 2022)](https://www.bangkokpost.com/life/arts-and-entertainment/2268655/giving-the-voiceless-a-voice)

<sub>says · about another work</sub>

> In Knit, I weave a spider web as a comparison to Greek mythology which tells the story of Arachne who was cursed to be a spider because she dared to criticise the god Athena. It is like when workers try to protest or speak up about their poor work conditions or low wage and they are threatened by people in power.
>
> — Kawita Vatanajyankur, [Bangkok Post, 'Giving the voiceless a voice' (Suwitcha Chaiyong, 2022)](https://www.bangkokpost.com/life/arts-and-entertainment/2268655/giving-the-voiceless-a-voice)

<sub>says · about another work</sub>

> Knitting is a form of work, a form of labour, but also a form of survival. To perform this work, I must use all parts of my body like the spider with its eight legs – I am being that machine.
>
> — Kawita Vatanajyankur, [AGWA / Simon Lee Foundation, 'My body as a domestic object', interview by Rachel Ciesla & Robert Cook](https://slficaa.artgallery.wa.gov.au/interview/my-body-as-a-domestic-object/)

<sub>says · about another work</sub>

> During the protest I saw a woman holding a sign that said ‘Are we machines?’.
>
> — Kawita Vatanajyankur, [AGWA / Simon Lee Foundation, 'My body as a domestic object', interview by Rachel Ciesla & Robert Cook](https://slficaa.artgallery.wa.gov.au/interview/my-body-as-a-domestic-object/)

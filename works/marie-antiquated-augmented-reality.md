# (antiquated) Augmented Reality

**Christine Marie**, 2019 · Light / Performance / body

(antiquated) Augmented Reality is a live performance directed by Christine Marie (producer Nion McEvoy) in which an ensemble of dancers (Taylor Unwin, Sandra Ruiz, Melissa Ferrari) performs as giant stereoscopic shadows, up to about 30 feet tall, cast with a non-digital, pre-cinematic stereo imaging technique. The audience views the shadows through anaglyph 3D glasses, so that the figures appear to step off the screen and reach into the room. It was shown in the Films and Performances part of the Sundance Film Festival's New Frontier programme in 2019.

## In their words

> In ANTIQUATED AUGMENTED REALITY, a flat feminine form transforms into a fully dimensional presence as she steps off and out of a screen. In this expanded media event, artist Christine Marie transforms a 2D shadow into 3D, while enacting a symbolic, kinetic departure from the media’s male gaze.
>
> — Christine Marie (artist's website), [Christine Marie, home page, 4christinemarie.com](https://www.4christinemarie.com/)

<sub>says · unsigned project text</sub>

> Engaging with the Fordist notion of the feminine form as a mass-produced consumer object, the work establishes choreographed, repetitive movements that are disrupted as the performer reaches into the z-axis—creating moments of intimacy and agency as she seemingly “touches” the audience.
>
> — Christine Marie (artist's website), [Christine Marie, home page, 4christinemarie.com](https://www.4christinemarie.com/)

<sub>says · unsigned project text</sub>

> She coined the terms "3D SHADOWS" & "Antiquated Augmented Reality," to describe this analog phenomenon, in which 2D shadows become volumetric forms in cubic space without the use of a computer.
>
> — Christine Marie (artist's website), [About, 4christinemarie.com](https://www.4christinemarie.com/about)

<sub>says · about their practice · unsigned project text</sub>

> I learned how to make the lights at ShadowLight, and I used a little of what I learned there, plus and a lot of advice from an LA stereoscopic society, to invent the 3D lights. I like that my hand has touched every part of the process. The lights are very much a handmade object. They're a part of the ensemble—another instrument.
>
> — Christine Marie, ['Shadowcaster Christine Marie Will Create 40-Foot Shadows During 'The Fifty Year Sword' This Halloween', LAist, 27 October 2011](https://laist.com/news/entertainment/shadowcaster-christine-marie)

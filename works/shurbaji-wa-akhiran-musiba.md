# Wa akhiran musiba

**Maya Shurbaji**, 2017 · Screen / monitor / Sound / Text / print

Wa akhiran musiba (At Last, a Tragedy) is a 15'49" video written, directed, shot and produced by Maya Shurbaji (editing Ayman Nahle, sound design Ziad Moukarzel). It opens with a WhatsApp exchange in Arabic between the artist and a friend, Louay, about life and death, followed by a man's voice describing sleep-deprivation torture in prison; through family footage and childhood images the non-linear narrative connects the imprisonment of the artist's father with her own history, including her gender transition, using archive material for Damascus and the past and footage from Beirut for the present. It premiered at the 68th Berlin Film Festival in 2018 and was shown at the 21st Bienal Sesc\_Videobrasil (2019-2020).

## In their words

> In my point of view, the political shapes up your personal life. I try to escape the collective tragedy by finding a personal and physical one, but without success, because it’s all connected, and once you deal with your personal pain you realize that it is a part of a bigger pain.
>
> — Maya Shurbaji, [Artist statement ('Depoimento'), Maya Shurbaji page, 21st Bienal Sesc\_Videobrasil website](https://bienalsescvideobrasil.org.br/edicao21/artista/maya-shurbaji)

> Patriarchy, dictatorship, operation, imprisonment are key subjects of the work At Last, that I present at the 21st Biennial, where I trace a non-linear narrative that takes the viewer through a tragic history.
>
> — Maya Shurbaji, [Artist statement ('Depoimento'), Maya Shurbaji page, 21st Bienal Sesc\_Videobrasil website](https://bienalsescvideobrasil.org.br/edicao21/artista/maya-shurbaji)

<sub>says</sub>

> When it comes to the technical realization of the movie, due to Syria’s political context, I couldn't film in my city, Damascus; instead I used personal archive when I was referring to the past or to my city. But it's part of my concept to resort to archive to talk about Syria and the past, whereas I used footage from Beirut to talk about the present.
>
> — Maya Shurbaji, [Artist statement ('Depoimento'), Maya Shurbaji page, 21st Bienal Sesc\_Videobrasil website](https://bienalsescvideobrasil.org.br/edicao21/artista/maya-shurbaji)

<sub>says</sub>

> For me, it's a constant loop—the past is the present and the present is the past. Time is passing but nothing is happening. I'm trapped. That's why I don't see a future, and that's why the future is not present in my film.
>
> — Maya Shurbaji, [Artist statement ('Depoimento'), Maya Shurbaji page, 21st Bienal Sesc\_Videobrasil website](https://bienalsescvideobrasil.org.br/edicao21/artista/maya-shurbaji)

# The Quiet Weight of Shadows

**Dhiraj Rabha**, 2025 · Sound / Screen / monitor / Material / sculpture

The Quiet Weight of Shadows is an installation shown at Aspinwall House in the Kochi-Muziris Biennale 2025-26 about the history of the ULFA insurgency in Assam. Visitors walk through a field of red- and green-lit artificial carnivorous plants that emit news broadcasts from the 1990s to 2010 in Assamese, Hindi and English, with watchtowers in which video interviews with former ULFA members play, and a reconstructed burnt house holding real archival newspapers, photographs, pamphlets and documents. The installation also screens the artist's film Whispers Beneath the Ashes (2025).

## In their words

> There is a mismatch between what was aired and what they actually say about their experience,
>
> — Dhiraj Rabha, [N.K. Bhoopesh, 'Where Memory Resists Power: The Many-Layered Histories in Dhiraj Rabha's Art', Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/culture-society/where-memory-resists-power-the-many-layered-histories-in-dhiraj-rabhas-art)

<sub>says</sub>

> From a distance, the flowers look beautiful. But when you come closer, there are layered stories inside. Videos are playing, voices are speaking. It looks different from far away, but closer, the contradictions appear. I am trying to portray the duality of media narratives,
>
> — Dhiraj Rabha, [Onmanorama, Kochi-Muziris Biennale report on Dhiraj Rabha (3 January 2026)](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2026/01/03/kochi-muziris-biennale-dhiraj-rabha-assam-insurgency-art-installation.html)

<sub>says</sub>

> I am trying to portray how the media narratives of the ULFA movement and the Assamese insurgency were cushioning the power dynamics, and how those in power controlled the narratives,
>
> — Dhiraj Rabha, [Onmanorama, Kochi-Muziris Biennale report on Dhiraj Rabha (3 January 2026)](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2026/01/03/kochi-muziris-biennale-dhiraj-rabha-assam-insurgency-art-installation.html)

<sub>says</sub>

> The contrast between the lighting and colour scheme of the carnivorous plants and the documents kept in the burnt house was deliberate,
>
> — Dhiraj Rabha, [The Federal, feature on The Quiet Weight of Shadows at the Kochi-Muziris Biennale](https://federaldbsite.hocalwire.in/category/features/kochi-muziris-biennale-assam-ulfa-insurgency-art-dhiraj-rabha-installation-plants-224563)

<sub>says</sub>

> In order to counter the toxicity that India's mainstream media outlets project about the Tribal Peoples of Assam, through sensationalized and biased stereotypes \[which their audiences accept, unquestioningly, as "the truth"\], I deliberately construct my installations with layers of poetic abstraction, such as through metaphors, local mythologies, and even fireflies to allow space for the viewer to reflect on our People's human suffering without feeling lectured to.
>
> — Dhiraj Rabha, [Cultural Survival, 'Surveilled Lives, Living Archives: Artist Dhiraj Rabha Retrieves History from the Shadows' (interview by Cristina Verán)](https://www.culturalsurvival.org/news/surveilled-livesliving-archives-artist-dhiraj-rabha-retrieves-history-shadows)

# Satan in America and Other Invisible Evils: Experiments in Public Sculpture (Witches 1–3)

**Isabelle Frances McGuire**, 2026 · Material / sculpture / Online / net art

Three witch figures in an installation shown at the Whitney Biennial 2026 that draws on scenes from American history, from the Salem witch trials to Abraham Lincoln's birth near Hodgenville, Kentucky. The witches' bodies are built from open-source medical 3D CT scans of human bodies, and two Salem villagers nearby are built from character models taken from the video game Doom. Visitors walk among figures mounted on metal poles alongside the artist's Symbolic Birth Cabin Unit and two demon figures.

## In their words

> This installation is really about insides and outsides and us versus them and hysteria and agony. I'm not trying to talk about witchcraft, the spiritual practice. I'm more trying to evoke the hysteria or the sense of fear or the possible parallels between Salem and now.
>
> — Isabelle Frances McGuire, [Whitney Biennial 2026 audio guide, stop 520](https://whitney.org/guides/139?language=en)

<sub>says</sub>

> It kind of also works like how science fiction is about the future, but it's normally about the present. So mine is a parallel world using these symbols, from the well of information and the well of free public virtual world.
>
> — Isabelle Frances McGuire, [Whitney Biennial 2026 audio guide, stop 520](https://whitney.org/guides/139?language=en)

<sub>says</sub>

> Something from a game space: the gift from the game Doom is a hell game space. So you kind of understand, very simply and easily, what this hell space might be like because we have a cultural similarity. And I think that's important for a sculptural conversation, which historically, sculptures are about, or have modeled, myths.
>
> — Isabelle Frances McGuire, [Whitney Biennial 2026 audio guide, stop 520](https://whitney.org/guides/139?language=en)

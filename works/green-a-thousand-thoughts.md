# A Thousand Thoughts

**Sam Green, Joe Bini**, 2018 · Projection / Sound / Performance / body

A Thousand Thoughts is a 'live documentary' portrait of the Kronos Quartet by Sam Green and Joe Bini, who are credited together with directing, writing and editing it. Green narrates live on stage while the Kronos Quartet performs the score, alongside archival footage, photos and interviews with the quartet and collaborators such as Philip Glass, Laurie Anderson, Terry Riley, Tanya Tagaq and Steve Reich. It premiered at the Sundance Film Festival's New Frontier section in 2018 and toured to venues including the Wexner Center and the Barbican Hall, London.

## In their words

> From the beginning, I felt like this form was perfect for a portrait of Kronos. They’ve been around for 40-plus years, have put out dozens of albums, collaborated with everyone from Astor Piazzolla to Laurie Anderson – the scale and scope of their oeuvre is incredible. But the most powerful thing about them really is hearing them play live.
>
> — Sam Green, [Robert Greene, 'A cinema-in-the-moment: Sam Green on A Thousand Thoughts and the ‘live documentary’', Sight and Sound (BFI), 2018](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/cinema-moment-sam-green-thousand-thoughts-live-documentary)

<sub>says</sub>

> So with these live documentary pieces, it’s super fun and challenging to toggle back and forth between these two modes of experience. At certain points in A Thousand Thoughts the viewer should be completely glued to the screen, but at other points we make you radically aware of being in the room and sharing this moment.
>
> — Sam Green, [Robert Greene, 'A cinema-in-the-moment: Sam Green on A Thousand Thoughts and the ‘live documentary’', Sight and Sound (BFI), 2018](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/cinema-moment-sam-green-thousand-thoughts-live-documentary)

<sub>says</sub>

> And with this piece in particular I knew that using some silence could be a powerful thing – to get the audience to really engage with their ears. With A Thousand Thoughts I knew I wanted people to listen in a different and much more engaged manner – that that would make the Quartet’s music much more of a profound experience.
>
> — Sam Green, [Robert Greene, 'A cinema-in-the-moment: Sam Green on A Thousand Thoughts and the ‘live documentary’', Sight and Sound (BFI), 2018](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/cinema-moment-sam-green-thousand-thoughts-live-documentary)

<sub>says</sub>

> For me it was important not to make a tribute film. That was something I wanted to avoid. It’s a portrait of them but it uses a portrait of them to explore bigger ideas, which I think was a good solution.
>
> — Sam Green, ['Q&A: Sam Green on A Thousand Thoughts', Wexner Center for the Arts blog, 2018](https://wexarts.org/blog/film-music-qa-sam-green-kronos-thousand-thoughts-world-premiere)

<sub>says</sub>

> So in the context of this new world, I feel strongly that there is a politics around the form of live cinema that’s important. The idea of us traveling all the way to London, you buying a ticket and all of us turning off our phones, being 100 percent present, and together creating something that will never happen the same way twice – there’s something not only magical about that, but there’s also something politically valuable too. Sitting in a dark room with strangers is a radical act!
>
> — Sam Green, [Robert Greene, 'A cinema-in-the-moment: Sam Green on A Thousand Thoughts and the ‘live documentary’', Sight and Sound (BFI), 2018](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/cinema-moment-sam-green-thousand-thoughts-live-documentary)

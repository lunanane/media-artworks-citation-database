# Dominus Trémulo

**Raúl Miranda**, 2017 · Projection / Sound / Performance / body

Dominus Trémulo is a 6-minute digital short film in black and white and colour directed by Raúl Miranda (performance by Jorge Santos, music by Sebastián Jatz), in which a man begins his morning ritual and is interrupted by a tremor, real or imagined. The work refers to the 1647 Santiago earthquake and the colonial legend of the Señor de Mayo, and to the ruins of the Convento do Carmo in Lisbon left by the 1755 earthquake. In the installation the film is projected in a loop onto a floating paper screen that vibrates from a sub-bass speaker mounted behind it, driven by music based on Purcell's 'Dido's Lament'.

## In their words

> During the three months I have been in Portugal, I have found myself facing several internal (emotional) earthquakes, as well as living in my constant “Chilean paranoia” regarding earthquakes and tsunamis.
>
> — Raúl Miranda, [Dominus Trémulo, work page, 13 Bienal de Artes Mediales, Santiago (translated from Spanish)](https://13.bienaldeartesmediales.cl/obra/dominus-tremulo/)

<sub>says · unsigned project text</sub>

> From this experience and observation in Portugal I take as working material the fact that in Chile we live day to day with disaster, because we have grown used to it and know how to act in the face of it, unlike the Portuguese, who have not experienced a catastrophic earthquake for 300 years and have forgotten, as a denial, the possibility of that horror.
>
> — Raúl Miranda, [Dominus Trémulo, work page, 13 Bienal de Artes Mediales, Santiago (translated from Spanish)](https://13.bienaldeartesmediales.cl/obra/dominus-tremulo/)

<sub>says · unsigned project text</sub>

> This work also proposes a contemporary reading of how we Chileans live day to day with imminent disaster (of whatever kind), since we have already grown used to a certain dose of suffering and know how to adapt to that state (resilience seen as a paraphilia, as a “sin” and not as a “virtue”).
>
> — Raúl Miranda (project text), [Dominus Trémulo, work page, 13 Bienal de Artes Mediales, Santiago (translated from Spanish)](https://13.bienaldeartesmediales.cl/obra/dominus-tremulo/)

> In this work, which offers multiple readings, the inclusion of the nude adds an emotional reaction to the restrictive social conditioning of the male body, as it underlines the vulnerability, discomfort and drama of the imminence of catastrophe.
>
> — Raúl Miranda (project text), [Dominus Trémulo, work page, 13 Bienal de Artes Mediales, Santiago (translated from Spanish)](https://13.bienaldeartesmediales.cl/obra/dominus-tremulo/)

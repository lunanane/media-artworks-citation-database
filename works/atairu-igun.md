# IGÚN

**Minne Atairu**, 2021 · AI / generative / Screen / monitor

An ongoing series of StyleGAN models trained on images of Benin Bronzes looted from the Benin Kingdom during the 1897 British invasion, now held in museum collections. It generates synthetic prototypes of objects that might have been cast during the 17-year interregnum (1897–1914), when no Oba commissioned work; datasets were extended with ceremonial heads in wood and pottery because there were too few bronze images to train the model. The Lumen Prize presentation shows video of latent-space interpolations; the work won the Lumen Prize Global South Award in 2021.

## In their words

> In this paper, I describe my artistic project, Igùn—a StyleGAN series trained to animate the research question: what bronze objects could have been produced should the 1897 British invasion not have occurred in the Benin Kingdom?
>
> — Minne Atairu, [Abstract, 'Reimagining Benin Bronzes using generative adversarial networks', AI & Society 39 (2024)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-023-01761-7)

<sub>asks</sub>

> Although post-invasion colonial reports referred to a thriving art scene and increased colonial art patronage, there is a dearth of visual documentation to identify objects created during this period. Considering this absence, I propose Igùn, a series of StyleGAN models trained on a dataset of looted Benin Bronzes.
>
> — Minne Atairu, [Abstract, 'Reimagining Benin Bronzes using generative adversarial networks', AI & Society 39 (2024)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-023-01761-7)

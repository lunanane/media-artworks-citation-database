# Das avós

**Rosana Paulino**, 2019 · Projection / Performance / body

Das avós is a video installation commissioned for the 21st Bienal Sesc\_Videobrasil (2019), Rosana Paulino's first work in video. In a dark room, looping projections show short performances in which a young woman (performer Charlene Bicalho) comes into caring contact with images of Black women from colonial and 19th-century Brazil; brief flashes of images of enslaved people also appear.

## In their words

> I thought mainly about who these women might be, and that one of them could possibly be my great-grandmother. Viewing them as matriarchs, as grandmothers, I reflected on what kind of affection I would like to show towards these ancestors—to cuddle them, offer them good food . . . in short, take care of these women.
>
> — Rosana Paulino, [Statement 2019 (transcription of the statement for the 21st Biennial), Videobrasil archive](https://site.videobrasil.org.br/en/acervo/artistas/textos/196468)

<sub>says</sub>

> The project was basically born from that idea of affection, and also from my reflecting on how important the issue of ancestry was, and still is, for the black population, I thought that viewing them as distant relatives would be a great work of personal and collective healing.
>
> — Rosana Paulino, [Statement 2019 (transcription of the statement for the 21st Biennial), Videobrasil archive](https://site.videobrasil.org.br/en/acervo/artistas/textos/196468)

<sub>says</sub>

> Individual memory lies in the possibility of having been close to two grandmothers and, therefore, thinking of sharing this experience with an “imaginary” great-grandmother. At the same time, in dealing with images that are in the public domain, I encroach the terrain of collectivity.
>
> — Rosana Paulino, [Statement 2019 (transcription of the statement for the 21st Biennial), Videobrasil archive](https://site.videobrasil.org.br/en/acervo/artistas/textos/196468)

<sub>says</sub>

> What I do is bring collective memory—which at times can be thought of as ancestry—to the core of the work. This is something that is barely explored in contemporary Brazilian production, including the production of black women.
>
> — Rosana Paulino, [Statement 2019 (transcription of the statement for the 21st Biennial), Videobrasil archive](https://site.videobrasil.org.br/en/acervo/artistas/textos/196468)

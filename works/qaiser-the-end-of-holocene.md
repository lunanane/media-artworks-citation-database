# THE END OF HOLOCENE: On Karachi's Islands… For The Record

**Tariq Alexander Qaiser**, 2024 · Screen / monitor / Camera / computer vision

A work shown at Lahore Biennale 03 drawn from about fifteen years of Qaiser's photographic and film recording in the mangrove forests of Bundal and Khiprianwala Islands, Korangi Creek, Karachi, documenting the forest and its cutting. It builds on more than 200 short films he has made and distributed weekly, with notes, as an open educational resource.

## In their words

> At first, I went there for pure pleasure and then I gradually began to notice changes. Where once there was a big tree, suddenly there is a stump and then several stumps and then a clearing was formed. That is when I decided to start documenting these forests and the changes I saw.
>
> — Tariq Alexander Qaiser, [Aurora (Dawn), interview with Mariam Ali Baig (Nov-Dec 2021)](https://aurora.dawn.com/news/1144283)

> It was a life form with which I had empathy for, and a relationship with.
>
> — Tariq Alexander Qaiser, [Aurora (Dawn), interview with Mariam Ali Baig (Nov-Dec 2021)](https://aurora.dawn.com/news/1144283)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> I kept this place to myself for years, just documenting it. However, I have realised that we cannot save these forests until there are enough people who want to save them, and people cannot be expected to save what they don’t know exists.
>
> — Tariq Alexander Qaiser, [Aurora (Dawn), interview with Mariam Ali Baig (Nov-Dec 2021)](https://aurora.dawn.com/news/1144283)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> Karachi is in crisis. We need a biodiverse wilderness if we are going to survive into the next few decades. This isn’t about saving the mangroves; this is about saving Karachi.
>
> — Tariq Alexander Qaiser, [TIME, 'The Miraculous Trees That Could Save Pakistan's Largest City from Climate Disaster' (2022)](https://time.com/6189898/mangrove-trees-pakistan-climate/)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> If you make it acceptable to destroy the forest right in front of the city, what happens when the city expands? It’ll become acceptable to use those islands too. Where does it stop? When the only mangroves left are the ones in the museum?
>
> — Tariq Alexander Qaiser, [TIME, 'The Miraculous Trees That Could Save Pakistan's Largest City from Climate Disaster' (2022)](https://time.com/6189898/mangrove-trees-pakistan-climate/)

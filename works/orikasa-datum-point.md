# Datum Point

**Ryo Orikasa**, 2015 · Screen / monitor / Material / sculpture / Text / print

A 6'41'' stop-motion clay animation based on the poem Suijun genten (Datum Point) by Yoshiro Ishihara (1915–77). Waves of white clay rise and swell, and the characters of the poem appear in them one at a time before being swallowed again; the artist shot it by hand over about a year, with music by Shun Owada. It screened in competition at Animafest Zagreb and Annecy in 2016.

## In their words

> He once wrote that writing a poem is like kneading clay. There’s a very physical resistance—you have to push through it. That idea of kneading, of wrestling with a lump of language, really stayed with me. Writing felt like a material struggle, and clay gave me a way to embody that in animation.
>
> — Ryo Orikasa, [Cartoon Brew, Animating Poetry (Chris Robinson)](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/animators/ryo-orikasa-animation-word-play-258351.html)

<sub>says</sub>

> No voice came to mind. There are recordings of him reciting his work, but I always felt that he wasn’t especially interested in performance. So for Datum Point, silence felt right. If I had forced a voice onto it, it would have betrayed that initial experience of reading—of not hearing.
>
> — Ryo Orikasa, [Cartoon Brew, Animating Poetry (Chris Robinson)](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/animators/ryo-orikasa-animation-word-play-258351.html)

<sub>says</sub>

> I think of the act of making the film as one continuous event, and the finished film is simply a recording of that event.
>
> — Ryo Orikasa, [Cartoon Brew, Animating Poetry (Chris Robinson)](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/animators/ryo-orikasa-animation-word-play-258351.html)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> I was interested in this film in the idea of a resistance to writing, of a resistance to something physical. In order to convey this motif of resisting the physical, in this case the physical act of writing, it seemed to me that the plasticity of clay could manifest this resistance to the physical more effectively than the act of writing on paper.
>
> — Ryo Orikasa, [Annecy 2016 Shorts & Breakfast, transcript on Backpacking Japan blog](https://backpackingjapan.blogspot.com/2016/06/annecy-2016-p-dej-du-court-shorts.html)

<sub>says</sub>

> This film is an attempt to seek out the landscape from his poem.
>
> — unattributed (festival synopsis), [Animafest Zagreb 2016 film page](https://animafest.hr/arhiva/en/2016/film/read/datum_point.html)

# The Maw Of

**Rachel Rossin**, 2022 · Online / net art / VR / AR / XR / Screen / monitor

A web-based animation with an augmented-reality layer, commissioned for the Whitney Museum's artport and co-commissioned by KW Institute for Contemporary Art, Berlin. In the browser, a ghostly female figure drawn in a manga style moves through layers of interfaces, elements of whitney.org, a graph of the nervous system, a web console, parental-guidance alerts and military infrared imagery; scanning an on-screen QR code opens an AR layer on the viewer's phone that adds text and moves the figure out of the browser onto the device. A site-specific version (five-channel video, LED screens, sculpture and a hand-tracking VR environment) was shown 15–18 September 2022 at the Tieranatomisches Theater, Berlin.

## In their words

> The commission was initially for a virtual space, and the approach was to discuss the black boxes around autonomy—where technology is going in regard to our autonomy, especially when it comes to physical sovereignty. Meaning, how is technology moving into the body? And what is the language we need to prepare around that?
>
> — Rachel Rossin, [Emily McDermott, "Shrinking Space" (interview), Berlin Art Week Journal, 6 September 2022](https://berlinartweek.de/en/article/shrinking-space/)

<sub>asks</sub>

> So, when I’m talking about autonomy, the framework is for us to regard technology as a peripheral for cognition, and the black boxes I’m specifically addressing in ›The Maw Of‹ have to do with this. Right now, technology is really separate from our bodies, but we’ll start to see the space between brain and machine interface, the space in a body, shrink and compress.
>
> — Rachel Rossin, [Emily McDermott, "Shrinking Space" (interview), Berlin Art Week Journal, 6 September 2022](https://berlinartweek.de/en/article/shrinking-space/)

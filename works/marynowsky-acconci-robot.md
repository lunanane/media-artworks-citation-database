# Acconci Robot

**Wade Marynowsky**, 2013 · Robotics / kinetics / Sensor / interaction

A robot in the form of a plain wooden crate that stays motionless while a visitor approaches, follows the visitor once they turn away, and stops when they look back. It refers to Vito Acconci's 1969 performance Following Piece; it was commissioned by Experimenta for its 5th International Biennale of Media Art (Speak to Me, 2012) and shown at ISEA2013 at the Powerhouse Museum, Sydney.

## In their words

> The Acconci robot is the third iteration of my research into robotics. I have stripped it back to look like a crate so people can project whatever notion they have of a robot onto it; and because people expected robots to respond to them in my previous work, in this case it does!
>
> — Wade Marynowsky, [Contemporary artefacts and narcissistic robots: an interview with Wade Marynowsky, NGV (2014)](https://www.ngv.vic.gov.au/essay/contemporary-artefacts-and-narcissistic-robots-an-interview-with-wade-marynowsky/)

<sub>says</sub>

> While my robots use abstracted human-like forms, I am actually interested in creating an uncanny visual effect.
>
> — Wade Marynowsky, [Contemporary artefacts and narcissistic robots: an interview with Wade Marynowsky, NGV (2014)](https://www.ngv.vic.gov.au/essay/contemporary-artefacts-and-narcissistic-robots-an-interview-with-wade-marynowsky/)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> In this cheeky and playful work, the artist inverts our expectations of the direct engagement and reciprocal exchange typical of interactivity, by creating a work that only responds when the audience is most disengaged from it.
>
> — Wade Marynowsky (artist's website, unsigned), [The Acconci Robot, artist's website](https://wademarynowsky.art/Acconci.html)

# Pinktoned (Exploded View)

**Mike Stoltz**, 2025 · Projection / Sound / Screen / monitor

A multi-screen installation shown in its own room at the Hammer Museum's Made in L.A. 2025. It presents the component parts of Stoltz's 16mm film Pinktoned separately: footage of passersby filmed from his East Hollywood studio, faded 1976 slides of Los Angeles streets wheat-pasted with posters for the documentary Underground, and a black-and-white moving graphic. A third screen runs a 16mm optical soundtrack of 'pink tone' test frequencies on a loop, and visitors see the street footage and the slides across the room from each other.

## In their words

> These 1976 images are juxtaposed with my own contemporary footage of passersby filmed out the window of my East Hollywood studio. Photographed in close up detail, these images show gestures of figures on the street; tapping out texts on touch screens, flicking ash off the end of a cigarette, gripping a coffee cup, passing money for bus fare.
>
> — Mike Stoltz, [EXiS (Experimental Film and Video Festival in Seoul), Pinktoned programme page](https://ex-is.org/ex-now/pinktoned)

<sub>says · unsigned project text</sub>

> Originally conceived as an installation for the Made in LA Biennial at the Hammer Museum, Pinktoned is a meditative passage through the streets of Los Angeles and a rumination on how moving images function amidst paranoia and state violence.
>
> — Mike Stoltz, [EXiS (Experimental Film and Video Festival in Seoul), Pinktoned programme page](https://ex-is.org/ex-now/pinktoned)

<sub>says · unsigned project text</sub>

> Working with the physical medium, every time you make a cut it becomes harder and harder to repair that. There’s not an undo button the way there is on the timeline of Adobe Premiere or Final Cut. Every edit is very deliberate and very final,
>
> — Mike Stoltz, [Binghamton News, 'Turning sounds into sights'](https://www.binghamton.edu/news/story/4559/turning-sounds-into-sights-from-making-music-to-the-movie-screen)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> I view sound recording as my sketchbook practice. With moving images, I usually need downtime between shooting. There are also many similarities between using cassette tape and shooting on film. They are both linear, tactile mediums.
>
> — Mike Stoltz, [Clint Enns, 'Re-Imagined Space: An Interview with Mike Stoltz', INCITE! (2014)](https://clintenns.ca/re-imagined-space/)

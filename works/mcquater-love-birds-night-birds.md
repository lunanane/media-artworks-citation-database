# Love Birds, Night Birds, Devil-Birds

**Cassie McQuater (original music by Kelly Moran)**, 2019 · Screen / monitor / Sensor / interaction

Love Birds, Night Birds, Devil-Birds is a nonlinear fairy tale made as an interactive video game installation and as a multi-channel video, with original music by Kelly Moran. It starts from Leonora Carrington's story 'The Debutante', in which a young woman exchanges places with a hyena masked in human skin, and shows dresses made of anatomical parts, glass and pink light moving through landscapes of fluorescent flowers, monsters and deconstructed birds. It debuted as a three-channel video installation in TRANSFER Gallery's series 'The Download', was released as a single-channel video with Warp Records, and won the Lumen Prize Moving Image Award in 2019.

## In their words

> “Love Birds, Night Birds, Devil-Birds” is a nonlinear fairy tale taking the form of an interactive video game installation, and begins as a re-imagining of the surrealist story “The Debutante” by artist Leonora Carrington, in which a young woman exchanges places with a hyena, masked in a suit of human skin, for her societal debut.
>
> — Cassie McQuater, [Work statement on the artist's website](https://www.cassiemcquater.com/lovebirds.html)

<sub>says</sub>

> It’s part of an on-going series exploring the mythological idea of women’s bodies as dangerous and poisonous gardens, leaning on an idea set forth in Nathaniel Hawthorne’s short story, “Rappaccini’s Daughter,” in which a young woman develops an inexplicable immunity to the poisonous plants which her father, a medical researcher, cultivates in medieval Padua.
>
> — Cassie McQuater, [Interview with Annamarie O'Brien, 'Interview with Video Game Artist Cassie McQuater', 29 April 2019](https://sites.psu.edu/annamarieobrien/2019/04/29/interview-with-video-game-artist-cassie-mcquater/)

<sub>says</sub>

> I use interactive storytelling, in the forms of video games or other new media installations, to examine and reflect on our relationships to narratives/narrative design, and how those narratives can influence us in the real world.
>
> — Cassie McQuater, [Interview with Annamarie O'Brien, 'Interview with Video Game Artist Cassie McQuater', 29 April 2019](https://sites.psu.edu/annamarieobrien/2019/04/29/interview-with-video-game-artist-cassie-mcquater/)

<sub>says</sub>

> I believe that the stories we tell ourselves influence as well as reflect back the type of society we live in, or the type of society we aim to live to in – fairy tales and folklore stories have a much wider cultural significance in their themes, morals and character interactions than they are typically given credit for in modern society.
>
> — Cassie McQuater, [Interview with Annamarie O'Brien, 'Interview with Video Game Artist Cassie McQuater', 29 April 2019](https://sites.psu.edu/annamarieobrien/2019/04/29/interview-with-video-game-artist-cassie-mcquater/)

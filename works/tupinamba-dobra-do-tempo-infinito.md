# Dobra do tempo infinito (Fold of Infinite Time)

**Glicéria Tupinambá, Atã Tupinambá Group & Tupinambá Community of Serra do Padeiro**, 2024 · Screen / monitor / Material / sculpture

Dobra do tempo infinito is a video installation shown in the Hãhãwpuá Pavilion (Brazil) at the 60th Venice Biennale, combining video with seeds, leaves, soil, fishing nets, jereré and samburá. The video records workshops Glicéria Tupinambá organised in the communities of Serra do Padeiro and Olivença (Bahia), in which masters made canoes, fishing nets and net-weaving needles and went fishing with trawls and tarrafas; the footage was shot by young people of the Atã Tupinambá Group with Augusto Santos (Method\_av). The venue text links the stitches of fishing nets to the weaving of traditional Tupinambá clothing.

## In their words

> Being with the young and the old, having a place to speak, passing on knowledge to the young, generates trust. The elders are the bearers of knowledge, and the transmission of knowledge by the masters in the village intensifies. Young people are more interested in wanting to learn, in wanting to understand the place of this knowledge. This space has been opened up to value the elders, and for younger people to seek out this knowledge in order to be custodians in the future,
>
> — Glicéria Tupinambá, [Fundação Bienal de São Paulo, 'Collectivity, autonomy and Indigenous foreigners at the Venice Biennale'](https://bienal.org.br/en/collectivity-autonomy-and-indigenous-foreigners-at-the-venice-biennale/)

<sub>says</sub>

> I don’t really create works of art. My work and my research are focused on rescuing the Tupinambá culture and their identity.
>
> — Glicéria Tupinambá, [ArtReview Venice pavilion questionnaire (2024)](https://artreview.com/gliceria-tupinamba-and-the-curators-of-the-hahawpua-pavilion-on-representing-brazil-at-the-60th-venice-biennale)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> I don’t think about a specific audience because the work is not made for an audience: it’s made for my community, it is a devolution for my people.
>
> — Glicéria Tupinambá, [ArtReview Venice pavilion questionnaire (2024)](https://artreview.com/gliceria-tupinamba-and-the-curators-of-the-hahawpua-pavilion-on-representing-brazil-at-the-60th-venice-biennale)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> I understand that if I were to look for a reference, I would look for the elders within the community. By understanding them, what they have of remains from a point, from a knot, they establish this influence very much. In this sense it would be my aunts and grandmothers.
>
> — Glicéria Tupinambá, [ArtReview Venice pavilion questionnaire (2024)](https://artreview.com/gliceria-tupinamba-and-the-curators-of-the-hahawpua-pavilion-on-representing-brazil-at-the-60th-venice-biennale)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> My job is to listen to and decipher fragments of Tupinambá history that have been previously told by others and not by ourselves, so that I can put them back together and finally tell our whole story for ourselves.
>
> — Glicéria Tupinambá, [Elephant interview (2024)](https://elephant.art/hahawpua-pavilions-artist-gliceria-tupinamba-so-much-of-our-heritage-is-trapped-on-european-ground)

# Sange Khara \[Hard Stone\]

**Laila Hida**, 2025 · Screen / monitor / Material / sculpture / Sound

Sange Khara \[Hard Stone\] is an installation developed for the 36th Bienal de São Paulo, anchored by a looping 16mm film of desert and oasis landscapes and figures drawn from cinematic references. Visitors sit or lie down under a tent-like canopy of melhifa cloth, a fabric worn by women in the Sahara, in a scenography based on nomadic structures of southern Morocco, surrounded by sound and light. It is a chapter of Hida's ongoing project Le Voyage du Phénix.

## In their words

> I’ve always been drawn to building spaces rather than objects. I’m interested in how we can experience art through the body—through rest, slowness, or stillness. In Sangeh Khara, viewers lie down under a canopy of melhifa fabric, surrounded by sound and light. The installation echoes nomadic architecture in the oases, where shade is essential. I wanted to offer a space that envelops the viewer—a place to breathe, to see differently.
>
> — Laila Hida, [ART AFRICA Magazine, 'Laila Hida: Sensing the Desert, Reimagining the Image' (interview by Brendon Bell-Roberts)](https://artafricamagazine.org/leila-hida-sensing-the-desert-reimagining-the-image/)

# SQUEEEQUE-SPEAKERDOME

**Alexis O'Hara**, 2011 · Sound / Material / sculpture / Sensor / interaction

A dome-shaped enclosure built from some 60–70 recycled speaker boxes facing inward, large enough for about half a dozen people. Visitors go inside, take one of four microphones hanging from the ceiling and speak or sing; their voices pass through effects (delay, phaser) and come back out of the speaker walls, so that strangers inside end up making sound together. First built for a performance at Centre des arts actuels Skol (Elektra festival, Montreal) in 2009, it toured festivals in Europe and Canada and became the first acquisition of HEK (Haus der Elektronischen Künste) Basel's media art collection; in 2021 the artist retitled it SQUEEEQUE-SPEAKERDOME and stopped using the word "igloo".

## In their words

> It’s a project that I proposed as a performance space for strangers to get involved in collaborative song
>
> — Alexis O'Hara, [Emily Raine, "Squeeeeque!: Alexis O'Hara's sonic igloo", Cult MTL, 28 November 2012](http://cultmontreal.com/2012/11/squeeeeque-alexis-oharas-sonic-igloo/)

> I’ve wired about 34 of the speakers, and when people go into the igloo, there are microphones hanging from the ceiling, and they can grab the mic and talk into it and the voices go through a delay and a phaser and then coming out of the walls of the speakerbox igloo.
>
> — Alexis O'Hara, [Emily Raine, "Squeeeeque!: Alexis O'Hara's sonic igloo", Cult MTL, 28 November 2012](http://cultmontreal.com/2012/11/squeeeeque-alexis-oharas-sonic-igloo/)

<sub>says</sub>

> An immersive environment, cosy as a children’s fort, built from items discarded by society. A sonic cocoon woven from the mutations of your own voice, reverberating across walls and ceiling.
>
> — Alexis O'Hara, [SQUEEEEQUE – SPEAKERDOME, project page on the artist's site (dyslex6.com)](http://www.dyslex6.com/lx6/projects/squeeeeque-the-improbable-igloo/)

# In Vitro

**Larissa Sansour**, 2019 · Projection / Sound

In Vitro is a two-channel, Arabic-language, black-and-white science-fiction film (about 28 minutes) by Larissa Sansour, co-directed with Søren Lind, commissioned by the Danish Arts Foundation for the Danish Pavilion at the 58th Venice Biennale (2019), where it was shown with the sculpture Monument for Lost Time under the title Heirloom. Set after an eco-disaster, it takes place in an abandoned nuclear reactor under Bethlehem converted into an orchard, where scientists prepare to replant the soil with heirloom seeds. The film is a dialogue between the dying founder of the bunker, Dunia, and her successor Alia, who was born underground and carries memories implanted in her.

## In their words

> In Vitro talks about nationalism in the context of the apocalypse, when the world is no longer as we know it. Does the vocabulary that we have or that we use for our nation-building or national identity really matter anymore?
>
> — Larissa Sansour, [Anna McNay, 'Larissa Sansour – interview', Studio International, 2019](https://www.studiointernational.com/larissa-sansour-interview-danish-pavilion-venice-biennale-2019)

<sub>asks</sub>

> It’s about people who carry their ancestors’ wishes and memories without having lived them. In this way, it’s addressing the trauma of Palestinians when they were kicked out of their country in 1948. How has this trauma defined the existence and identity of Palestinians today?
>
> — Larissa Sansour, [Anna McNay, 'Larissa Sansour – interview', Studio International, 2019](https://www.studiointernational.com/larissa-sansour-interview-danish-pavilion-venice-biennale-2019)

<sub>asks</sub>

> I want to present the fact that there is a problem with holding on to the past and memories.
>
> — Larissa Sansour, [Anna McNay, 'Larissa Sansour – interview', Studio International, 2019](https://www.studiointernational.com/larissa-sansour-interview-danish-pavilion-venice-biennale-2019)

<sub>says</sub>

> I feel that is often what identity and trauma does to you: you become stuck in an idea that you can’t get out of without losing yourself. It becomes a hindrance to moving on.
>
> — Larissa Sansour, [Anna McNay, 'Larissa Sansour – interview', Studio International, 2019](https://www.studiointernational.com/larissa-sansour-interview-danish-pavilion-venice-biennale-2019)

<sub>says</sub>

> It’s more a sense of questioning what kind of vocabulary we need to have in order to face the future, because things are changing and what is most important is not being addressed, and we are still playing a game that is about to become obsolete.
>
> — Larissa Sansour, [Anna McNay, 'Larissa Sansour – interview', Studio International, 2019](https://www.studiointernational.com/larissa-sansour-interview-danish-pavilion-venice-biennale-2019)

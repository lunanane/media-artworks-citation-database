# OPERA

**Erick Oh**, 2021 · Screen / monitor / Projection / Sound

An 8K animation conceived as an installation: a looping cycle of about five minutes showing a giant pyramid filled with hundreds of small black-and-white figures in a hierarchical society, passing through day and night, modelled on Renaissance fresco murals. The installation version has no dialogue and no camera movement; visitors walk along the image and choose which characters and sections to follow. A cinema version was nominated for the 2021 Academy Award for Animated Short Film, and the work received an Award of Distinction in Computer Animation at Prix Ars Electronica 2021.

## In their words

> Opera definitely started from the subject matter. I wanted to showcase a reflection of human society and history — I immediately thought about what would be the best way to convey our life, and realized that maybe following the conventional, linear narrative structure may not be the best way.
>
> — Erick Oh, [Erick Oh On ‘Opera’ (Oscar Shorts Interview Series), Cartoon Brew, April 2021](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/awards/erick-oh-on-opera-oscar-shorts-interview-series-204262.html)

<sub>says</sub>

> Conventional film language has the beginning and the end, a climax. History doesn’t work that way. Our life is one continuous rhythm, so that’s why I came up with this structure of the cycle, so we can watch this numerous times and embrace everything happening.
>
> — Erick Oh, [Cartoon Brew, Oscar Shorts Interview Series, 2021](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/awards/erick-oh-on-opera-oscar-shorts-interview-series-204262.html)

<sub>says</sub>

> Then the other one — that doesn’t have any camera movement — you just walk in, you see this huge pyramid structure. And then it’s really up to you to walk around and get closer and see the ending and the beginnings of each section all blended together.
>
> — Erick Oh, [Cartoon Brew, Oscar Shorts Interview Series, 2021](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/awards/erick-oh-on-opera-oscar-shorts-interview-series-204262.html)

<sub>says</sub>

> I think it simply comes from looking at the world and cycles of life and knowing you can’t control it. It constantly evolves and changes and transforms, and comes back to where it used to be.
>
> — Erick Oh, [Cartoon Brew, Oscar Shorts Interview Series, 2021](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/awards/erick-oh-on-opera-oscar-shorts-interview-series-204262.html)

<sub>says</sub>

> “Opera” itself doesn’t give you any answer. It’s almost like a mirror to ourselves. In the piece, you’ll find the world, all the social issues we are suffering with, life and death and eventually yourself. “Opera” doesn’t spoon feed you the message of hope. But it’ll have to be you who should take a moment to think and find a way to break out of this vicious cycle of repetitive history and make the world better.
>
> — Erick Oh, [Interview with Jackson Murphy, Animation Scoop (email Q&A), 2021](https://animationscoop.com/?p=12735)

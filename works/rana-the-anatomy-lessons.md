# The Anatomy Lessons

**Rashid Rana**, 2012 · Screen / monitor

A single-channel video by Rashid Rana, dated 2011–2013, shown at Aspinwall House, Fort Kochi, as part of the Kochi-Muziris Biennale. The biennale's Google Arts & Culture entry places it in a body of work in which Rana addresses divides between East and West, static and movement, and formal and conceptual. Project support is credited to Chatterjee & Lal and Chemould Prescott Road, Mumbai.

## In their words

> In the new body of works Rashid examines the divides between East and West; static and movement; formal and conceptual; all elements that have turned into clichés. Rana approaches the question of split and whole on multiple levels, which also include the presence of immediate physical surroundings and the experience of remote reality through books, TV, Internet, past and art history.
>
> — Kochi-Muziris Biennale (unsigned), [The Anatomy Lessons – Kochi-Muziris Biennale on Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/the-anatomy-lessons-rashid-rana/cwF6XN-DJEEOMQ)

<sub>says · unsigned project text</sub>

> I believe that the binaries of East and West are often overplayed. The binaries of ‘actual’ and ‘remote’ are more plausible in this regard; one’s expression is a result of a negotiation between the ‘actual’ and the ‘remote’. The actual is close at hand – something one can experience directly with the body as the site of knowing. The remote is knowledge amassed indirectly, from diverse sources scattered across time and space.
>
> — Rashid Rana, ['Portraits of possibility', interview by e-mail, Arts Illustrated](https://artsillustrated.in/?p=14238)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> I think every image, idea and truth encompasses its opposite within itself. Thus, we can say that we live in a state of duality. These dualities seem to exist separately but are inseparable when inspected closely. My intent is not to try and separate the polar opposites but it is rather to bring them on a singular plane for the viewer to inspect and form connections,
>
> — Rashid Rana, ['Portraits of possibility', interview by e-mail, Arts Illustrated](https://artsillustrated.in/?p=14238)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> Every image has its own historical context and baggage. I wanted to liberate myself and my viewer from the specificity we give to any one image
>
> — Rashid Rana, ['Rashid Rana: transfiguring history', The Art Newspaper (15 March 2017)](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2017/03/15/rashid-rana-transfiguring-history)

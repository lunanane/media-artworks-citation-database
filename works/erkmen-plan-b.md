# Plan B

**Ayşe Erkmen**, 2011 · Material / sculpture / Sound

Plan B was the Pavilion of Turkey at the 54th Venice Biennale (Artiglierie, Arsenale, 4 June–27 November 2011), curated by Fulya Erdemci: a working water-purification unit whose components were separated and spread through a room with a window onto the canal, reconnected by extended pipes painted in colours according to their function. Canal water was piped in, desalinated and purified to drinking quality, and returned to the canal; visitors stood among the machines and their noise while the water itself stayed out of sight inside the pipes.

## In their words

> This window to water told me that I had to find a way to bring it into this room one way or other. The canals of Venice that surrounds the whole city gave me the form and informed me that the room should be like the city itself surrounded by water. On the other hand the water needed to have an aim to come into this space. The most common thought about water is to drink it.
>
> — Ayşe Erkmen, [Jade Removille, “Ayşe Erkmen: Unhooked meanings transcending the worlds of architecture and spatial design,” NR magazine](https://nr.world/ayse-erkmen/)

<sub>says</sub>

> My first idea to make the visitors drink the water was given up and the title therefore became Plan B. Fulya Erdemci and I had a last minute thought that making people drink water out of this work would be a too easy gesture and make the work too popular and take it out of its real content.
>
> — Ayşe Erkmen, [Jade Removille, “Ayşe Erkmen: Unhooked meanings transcending the worlds of architecture and spatial design,” NR magazine](https://nr.world/ayse-erkmen/)

<sub>says</sub>

> I was and am still interested in purposelessness. I am always trying to achieve the most unhooked sense that aims to make the work be far from serving a reason or expectation.
>
> — Ayşe Erkmen, [Jade Removille, “Ayşe Erkmen: Unhooked meanings transcending the worlds of architecture and spatial design,” NR magazine](https://nr.world/ayse-erkmen/)

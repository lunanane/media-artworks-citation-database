# Corpus Nil

**Marco Donnarumma**, 2016 · Performance / body / Sound / AI / generative

Corpus Nil is a 20-25 minute performance for one human performer and artificial-intelligence algorithms. In a dark space, Donnarumma, partly naked and painted black, performs a slow choreography while biosensors (chip microphones for muscle sounds and EMG electrodes) send data to software that learns his movement and chooses on its own how to generate multichannel sound and light, which in turn affects his movement. It premiered on 6 February 2016 at ZKM Karlsruhe and received an Award of Distinction in Digital Musics & Sound Art at Prix Ars Electronica 2017.

## In their words

> In Corpus Nil, I wanted to profane the assumption of the body as a pure, whole entity and create a tangible experience of hybridity.
>
> — Marco Donnarumma, [Ars Electronica Blog, '“Corpus Nil”: A Performance by Man and Artificial Intelligence' (interview, 4 September 2017)](https://ars.electronica.art/aeblog/en/2017/09/04/corpus-nil/)

> What is important, though, is that the loop cannot be controlled; I cannot predict what exactly the machine will do, and the machine cannot predict how exactly I’ll move.
>
> — Marco Donnarumma, [Ars Electronica Blog, '“Corpus Nil”: A Performance by Man and Artificial Intelligence' (interview, 4 September 2017)](https://ars.electronica.art/aeblog/en/2017/09/04/corpus-nil/)

<sub>says</sub>

> And if there’s an aim in this artwork – and more generally in my whole body of works – it is exactly that: to create a performative experience that cannot be immediately rationalised, but instead hits you fast and hard so that it remains vivid in your memories.
>
> — Marco Donnarumma, [Ars Electronica Blog, '“Corpus Nil”: A Performance by Man and Artificial Intelligence' (interview, 4 September 2017)](https://ars.electronica.art/aeblog/en/2017/09/04/corpus-nil/)

<sub>says</sub>

> I want to tear open the stereotypes and prejudices we all have towards different bodies and identities. And I want to show the potential of AI to do that.
>
> — Marco Donnarumma, [Ars Electronica Blog, '“Corpus Nil”: A Performance by Man and Artificial Intelligence' (interview, 4 September 2017)](https://ars.electronica.art/aeblog/en/2017/09/04/corpus-nil/)

<sub>says</sub>

> Human bodies and identities are continuously categorized, online and offline, by artificially intelligent algorithms and machines. But what if, by contrast, artificial intelligence could be used to contaminate human bodily experience? How does a body defiled by algorithms look and move like?
>
> — Marco Donnarumma, [marcodonnarumma.com, 'Corpus Nil' work page (credits name Donnarumma as author of the artwork's description texts)](https://marcodonnarumma.com/works/corpus-nil/)

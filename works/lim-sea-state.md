# SEA STATE

**Charles Lim**, 2015 · Screen / monitor / Material / sculpture / Text / print

A multi-part project begun in 2005 about Singapore's relationship to the sea, structured on the World Meteorological Organization's numbered sea-state scale. Its presentation at the Singapore Pavilion of the 56th Venice Biennale (2015) included a barnacle-covered buoy replica, archival material and maps, photographs, a large installation mapping Singapore's sea floor, and films on land reclamation and sand extraction.

## In their words

> I am inverting the way we look at water. Water is often seen as a barricade. Somehow, the dominant myth about it is that if you fall into the water, you only have 15 seconds – or something like that – to live, before hypothermia kicks in. But in the tropics, the temperature of water is near to the temperature of our blood. Water is actually safe; it is a space where one may seek refuge in case of a storm.
>
> — Charles Lim, [ArtReview Asia, Summer 2015 feature](https://artreview.com/summer-2015-ara-feature-charles-lim/)

<sub>says</sub>

> Often, one encounters the approach that positions the sea as the sublime; it is at the sea that one finds the transcendental; that the sea is limitless; a flat space onto which one may project all of one’s desires. I feel this is quite dangerous. What has driven SEA STATE, right from the outset, has been this attempt to resist what I call the ‘romanticisation of the sea’.
>
> — Charles Lim, [ArtReview Asia, Summer 2015 feature](https://artreview.com/summer-2015-ara-feature-charles-lim/)

> I do not dwell in moral positionalities

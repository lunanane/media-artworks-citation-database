# Requiem for an Exit

**Frode Oldereid, Thomas Kvam**, 2025 · Robotics / kinetics / Projection / Sound

An installation centred on a four-metre-tall armless robotic figure on a steel and hydraulic structure, whose hyperrealistic face is a projected animation built from a motion-capture recording of Thomas Kvam. In a darkened room with multichannel sound, the robot delivers a monologue written by the artists and voiced with AI speech synthesis, tracing genocide through human history from the disappearance of the Neanderthals; there is no interactivity. First shown at Meta.Morf 2024 in Trondheim (co-produced by TEKS); Golden Nica, New Animation Art, Prix Ars Electronica 2025.

## In their words

> What interests us is what we inherit, not just genetically, but symbolically and ethically. The question is: What do we carry forward—consciously or not? The monologue doesn’t answer—it destabilizes. It turns the body into an archive and asks us to listen.
>
> — Thomas Kvam, [Ars Electronica Blog, 'Words as Weapons' (Katia Kreuzhuber), 7 July 2025](https://ars.electronica.art/aeblog/?p=80241)

> Some might interpret this as biopolitical determinism, but our intention is quite the opposite—to dismantle the idea of simple explanations. There is no app against evil.
>
> — Frode Oldereid, [Ars Electronica Blog, 'Words as Weapons' (Katia Kreuzhuber), 7 July 2025](https://ars.electronica.art/aeblog/?p=80241)

> Rather than asking who we are, the work asks how many times we will be the same. It confronts repetition itself—those inherited patterns looping through history. But within that cycle lies a question—or perhaps an opening: Can we become something other than what we were?
>
> — Frode Oldereid, [Ars Electronica Blog, 'Words as Weapons' (Katia Kreuzhuber), 7 July 2025](https://ars.electronica.art/aeblog/?p=80241)

<sub>asks</sub>

> And we are easily deceived: a few words in the right order, a sigh in the right place—and we think we’re talking to something alive. We exploit that weakness. It’s an existential question in technological disguise.
>
> — Thomas Kvam, [Ars Electronica Blog, 'Words as Weapons' (Katia Kreuzhuber), 7 July 2025](https://ars.electronica.art/aeblog/?p=80241)

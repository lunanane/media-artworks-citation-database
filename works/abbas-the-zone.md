# The Zone

**Basel Abbas & Ruanne Abou-Rahme**, 2011 · Screen / monitor / Sound / Material / sculpture

The Zone is an installation by Basel Abbas and Ruanne Abou-Rahme consisting of a built structure, two-channel video and four-channel sound, with 4–8 minute video and sound clips on 15 LCD screens. Visitors move through its narrow corridors and a room; the project's research documents advertisements, new commercial and residential developments, old political posters and tunnels and walls in the West Bank. It is part of an ongoing research project that also includes photographic series (Desire and Disaster, The Tunnel Series), an online platform and a publication, and was shown at the New Art Exchange and the 5th Jerusalem Show.

## In their words

> In one of the darkest moments in Palestinian lived history, a ‘dream-world’ has somehow emerged in the West Bank: a host of commodified desires, a semblance of normality, have been constructed atop the debris of political failure and collapse.
>
> — Basel Abbas and Ruanne Abou-Rahme (artists' website), [The Zone, work page, baselandruanne.com](https://baselandruanne.com/The-Zone)

<sub>says · unsigned project text</sub>

> Constructed as an immersive environment, a site of ruin and dream, a physical reflection on a subjectivity marked by a double moment of colonial expansion/political defeat and impending statehood/consumptive regime.
>
> — Basel Abbas and Ruanne Abou-Rahme (artists' website), [The Zone, work page, baselandruanne.com](https://baselandruanne.com/The-Zone)

<sub>says · unsigned project text</sub>

> The research for the project explores the particular dynamics that have brought about the construction of a consumerist regime out of the remains of an aborted Palestinian struggle. In previous works we have looked at the failure of the Palestinian resistance movement, but for this project, we wanted to focus on what happened after the failure: the transformation of the PLO into an ‘authority’ and eventually a ‘security’ regime.
>
> — Ruanne Abou-Rahme & Basel Abbas, [Ruanne Abourahme & Basel Abbas, 'The Zone', Designing Civic Encounter, ArtTerritories (archived 2016)](https://web.archive.org/web/20160401174654/http://www.artterritories.net/designingcivicencounter/?page_id=633)

> What struck us as most significant is the way in which this new regime displaced the old collective ‘dreams’ and gave birth to new political discourses and desires largely centered on consumption.

# Laundry Nocturne

**Kingsley Ng**, 2026 · Projection / Sound

Laundry Nocturne (2026) is a multi-channel video projection with field recordings, shown in Fermata: Hong Kong in Venice, the Hong Kong collateral exhibition of the 61st Venice Biennale curated by the Hong Kong Museum of Art. Silhouettes of laundry hung between buildings are projected in the room in a way that simulates moonlight entering through the space's windows. A soundtrack of night-time recordings from Hong Kong, including a wastewater treatment plant, a newspaper-printing facility and a postal sorting centre, plays from a radio in the room.

## In their words

> In a world saturated by ceaseless stimuli, I often ask whether urban media art can do more than dazzle—whether it might return us to a finer state of feeling. Sound, light or rhythm, I am drawn to small, almost imperceptible interventions: gestures slight enough to let perception open slowly, and encounters flourish naturally.
>
> — Kingsley Ng, [ArtDog Istanbul, 'Fermata: Hong Kong in Venice' (interview with the artists and curators)](https://artdogistanbul.com/en/fermata-hong-kong-in-venice/)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> The central motif is fluttering laundry, which charts rhythms of everyday life in both cities. The accompanying audio is also grounded in the quotidian, with field recordings in night-time Hong Kong (which is day time Venice during which the exhibition opens), including tribute to those who prepare the city for tomorrow while most are asleep, and other sounds of intimate domesticity.
>
> — Kingsley Ng, [ArtDog Istanbul, 'Fermata: Hong Kong in Venice' (interview with the artists and curators)](https://artdogistanbul.com/en/fermata-hong-kong-in-venice/)

<sub>says</sub>

> My work always revolves around the notion of impermanence. I don’t really see my practice as preservation or transformation. It is more like an acknowledgement of ephemerality and seizing a fleeting moment.
>
> — Kingsley Ng, [ArtDog Istanbul, 'Fermata: Hong Kong in Venice' (interview with the artists and curators)](https://artdogistanbul.com/en/fermata-hong-kong-in-venice/)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> The laundry becomes a point of entry into a wider soundscape of nighttime labour: recordings from a wastewater treatment plant, a newspaper-printing facility and a postal sorting centre evoke the work that continues while much of the city sleeps
>
> — Kingsley Ng, [The Art Newspaper, 'Hong Kong artists create a poetic moment in Venice' (4 November 2026)](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/11/04/hong-kong-artists-create-a-poetic-moment-in-venice)

<sub>says</sub>

> Creating spaces for listening has always been at the core of my practice. When I work with intangible media like light and sound, the absence of material subjects goes hand in hand with a preference for not approaching art as an amplifier for personal statements, but as an open environment for tuning in to the unsung and holding space for a less fixed, more receptive state of mind.
>
> — Kingsley Ng, [ArtReview, 'Angel Hui and Kingsley Ng on Representing Hong Kong at the 61st Venice Biennale' (questionnaire)](https://artreview.com/angel-hui-and-kingsley-ng-on-representing-hong-kong-at-the-61st-venice-biennale/)

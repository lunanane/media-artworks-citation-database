# Interface I

**Ralf Baecker**, 2016 · Robotics / kinetics / Material / sculpture

A kinetic installation in which 192 DC motors in two facing rows pull vertical strings against each other like a tug of war, while a mesh of red elastic bands couples each string to its neighbours. The motors are driven by random impulses from Geiger-Müller tubes that register natural background radiation, so the shape of the mesh keeps shifting without a programmed choreography. Visitors watch the band network move as the opposing forces push it up or down. The work was first shown as Order+Noise (Interface I) at NOME Gallery, Berlin, in 2016.

## In their words

> In case of Interface I it is one way of feeding a system with entropy (chance) and an external rhythm, caused by the unpredictable radioactive decay of elements.
>
> — Ralf Baecker, [Régine Debatty, "Order+Noise, a tug of war for motors, strings and rubber bands" (Q&A with the artist), We Make Money Not Art, 29 April 2016](https://we-make-money-not-art.com/ordernoise-a-tug-of-war-for-motors-strings-and-rubber-bands/)

<sub>says</sub>

> So the noise acts like a catalyst. From complexity theory we know the principle of self-organisation or “Order from Noise”. Whereby spontaneous order can arise by random fluctuations, like the flocking of birds or in an economy.
>
> — Ralf Baecker, [Régine Debatty, "Order+Noise, a tug of war for motors, strings and rubber bands" (Q&A with the artist), We Make Money Not Art, 29 April 2016](https://we-make-money-not-art.com/ordernoise-a-tug-of-war-for-motors-strings-and-rubber-bands/)

<sub>says</sub>

> For Interface I was trying to imagine the tiny and rapid interactions and transactions of a communication between two separate structures. How do they meet in one point and develop a language and get entangled it some kind of dialog.
>
> — Ralf Baecker, [Régine Debatty, "Order+Noise, a tug of war for motors, strings and rubber bands" (Q&A with the artist), We Make Money Not Art, 29 April 2016](https://we-make-money-not-art.com/ordernoise-a-tug-of-war-for-motors-strings-and-rubber-bands/)

<sub>says</sub>

> I don’t think that the machines become more like us, it is more likely that we adapt to the languages of the machines, to become computable.
>
> — Ralf Baecker, [Régine Debatty, "Order+Noise, a tug of war for motors, strings and rubber bands" (Q&A with the artist), We Make Money Not Art, 29 April 2016](https://we-make-money-not-art.com/ordernoise-a-tug-of-war-for-motors-strings-and-rubber-bands/)

> Another important point, is that I don’t follow the usual separation between process and output. In most of my machines display and process are one, the display is where the process happens and at the same time it is indicating it.

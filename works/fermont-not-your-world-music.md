# Not Your World Music: Noise in South East Asia

**Cedrik Fermont, Dimitri della Faille**, 2017 · Text / print / Sound

Not Your World Music: Noise in South East Asia is a 276-page book with a companion CD compilation on noise, electroacoustic, industrial and experimental music and sound art in Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand and Vietnam. It contains historical, political and sociological essays, interviews with artists and organisers, a bibliography of popular music from the region and a discography; the compilation features exclusive tracks by twelve artists from the region. It was published in September 2016 by Syrphe (Berlin) and Hushush (Ottawa) under a Creative Commons licence and is also available as a free PDF.

## In their words

> The book — just as my essays and talks — is partly there to disprove this myth. And the title is clear about it. Most noise artists don’t use traditional elements in their music, wherever they live on Earth, so why would Asians or Africans break the rule to fit their ex-colonizers’ expectations?
>
> — Cedrik Fermont, [Zuzana Friday, 'Digging the Asian and African undergrounds with C-drík Kirdec', CDM Create Digital Music (August 2016)](https://cdm.link/2016/08/digging-asian-african-undergrounds/)

<sub>asks</sub>

> I think it would be hard to understand why noise music exists or not somewhere without historical and socio-political and sometimes religious or philosophical context.
>
> — Cedrik Fermont, [Zuzana Friday, 'Digging the Asian and African undergrounds with C-drík Kirdec', CDM Create Digital Music (August 2016)](https://cdm.link/2016/08/digging-asian-african-undergrounds/)

<sub>says</sub>

> It is partly the result of a frustration and partly the result of many years of exploration, research, travels and discussions.
>
> — Cedrik Fermont, ['Interview with Cedrik Fermont (C-Drik) about Less Familiar Underground Scenes In The World', Side-Line (2016)](http://www.side-line.com/interview-with-cedrik-fermont-c-drik-about-less-familiar-underground-scenes-in-the-world-why-am-i-one-of-the-few-africans-in-this-scene-2/)

<sub>says</sub>

> I think that all my researches, travels, explorations, publications and releases about Asian and Africa come from the fact that I am tired to always hear the same non-sense: ‘There’s nothing there’ or ‘We – the West – invented everything before them’ or ‘They just copy music from the West’, etc.
>
> — Cedrik Fermont, ['Interview with Cedrik Fermont (C-Drik) about Less Familiar Underground Scenes In The World', Side-Line (2016)](http://www.side-line.com/interview-with-cedrik-fermont-c-drik-about-less-familiar-underground-scenes-in-the-world-why-am-i-one-of-the-few-africans-in-this-scene-2/)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> This compilation is an attempt to cover a varied range of noise and experimental music from South East Asia. Limited to just over one hour, the CD format does not allow us to fully cover the richness of the scene. However, we did our best to include a diverse group of artists. We have selected female and male artists, DIY and academically trained musicians, and newcomers and established artists.
>
> — Cedrik Fermont and Dimitri della Faille (project website), [Not Your World Music, 'Companion compilation' page, words.hushush.com](http://words.hushush.com/NOT_YOUR_WORLD_MUSIC_Noise_in_South_East_Asia_-_compilation.html)

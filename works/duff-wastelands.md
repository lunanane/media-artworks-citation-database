# Wastelands

**Tagny Duff**, 2018 · Material / sculpture

Wastelands is a biological art project that speculates on a future 500 years from now in which humans fuel the world with biogas made from their own waste. It consists of small biogas generator sculptures in glass and bioplastic housing methanogenic bacteria, archaea and bacteriophages, developed with anaerobic-digestion researchers at Michigan State University. It was shown at the Broad Art Museum, MSU (2018-2019).

## In their words

> I am interested in working with ruminal methangenic bacteria and archaea, microbes that thrive in oxygen-free environments like the guts of cows, sheep and humans.
>
> — Tagny Duff, [Wastelands project blog (tagnyduff.blog), 'Wastelands Project Overview', October 2017, Wayback Machine copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20190920211119/https://tagnyduff.blog/2017/10/12/first-blog-post/)

<sub>says · unsigned project text</sub>

> What if this new/old tech was scaled down for personal use? What kind of lifestyles might emerge? How might humans see themselves when depending on their own waste and the interaction with microbes to survive?
>
> — Tagny Duff, [Wastelands project blog, 'Wastelands Project Overview' (2017)](https://web.archive.org/web/20190920211119/https://tagnyduff.blog/2017/10/12/first-blog-post/)

<sub>asks · unsigned project text</sub>

> This idea is also deliberately rather absurd since from a societal point of view, cleanliness (and aseptic technique) is the dominate ideology of the day. Who, today, would want to carry poop and bacteria on their body?
>
> — Tagny Duff, [Wastelands project blog, 'Wastelands Project Overview' (2017)](https://web.archive.org/web/20190920211119/https://tagnyduff.blog/2017/10/12/first-blog-post/)

<sub>asks · unsigned project text</sub>

> What if this “green” tech considered alternative relations between human-animal-microbial interactions that are not based on utilitarian and capital driven results? What if this new/old tech was scaled down and diversified to slow the pace of tech development?
>
> — Tagny Duff, [Prix Ars Electronica archive, catalogue text](https://archive.aec.at/prix/245484/)

# Twin Quasar

**Ashley Zelinskie**, 2024 · VR / AR / XR / Online / net art

Twin Quasar is a three-dimensional virtual artwork and space in the Whitney Museum Virtual Landscape, commissioned for the Whitney's artport. It turns two works from the Whitney's collection, László Moholy-Nagy's Space Modulator (1938–40) and Rosalind Bengelsdorf Browne's Compotier II (1938), into navigable 3D models whose forms protrude from the virtual canvas as visitors approach, in an experience that replicates gravitational lensing. It can be visited on the multi-user MONA platform, where visitors see and can talk to each other, and in the Apple Vision Pro.

## In their words

> Gravitational lensing has fascinated me since 2022, when the first images from the James Webb Space Telescope were revealed.
>
> — Ashley Zelinskie, [Lara Pan, 'From Twin Quasars to Digital Frontiers at the Whitney Artport Whitney Virtual Space', Whitehot Magazine (22 May 2025)](https://whitehotmagazine.com/articles/virtual-space-by-lara-pan/7026)

> The concept of light being bent and magnified by an invisible intermediary force felt like a powerful metaphor to explore artistically as part of Twin Quasar.
>
> — Ashley Zelinskie, [Lara Pan, Whitehot Magazine (22 May 2025)](https://whitehotmagazine.com/articles/virtual-space-by-lara-pan/7026)

<sub>says</sub>

> In a way, this is an invisible sculpture—without the color and light of the paintings, there would be nothing to see. Only through the light being bent and distorted does the gravitational effect reveal itself.
>
> — Ashley Zelinskie, [Lara Pan, Whitehot Magazine (22 May 2025)](https://whitehotmagazine.com/articles/virtual-space-by-lara-pan/7026)

<sub>says</sub>

> Only with VR could I hope to recreate the sensation of looking through a gravitational lens, or even navigating the warped space-time it represents.
>
> — Ashley Zelinskie, [Lara Pan, Whitehot Magazine (22 May 2025)](https://whitehotmagazine.com/articles/virtual-space-by-lara-pan/7026)

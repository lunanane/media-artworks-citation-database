# 2.6g 329m/s (Bulletproof Skin)

**Jalila Essaïdi**, 2011 · Material / sculpture

2.6g 329m/s, also known as Bulletproof Skin, is a bio-art project in which in vitro human skin was reinforced with spider silk produced by genetically modified organisms (transgenic goats and silkworms) and then shot at with .22 calibre Long Rifle bullets; the title is the bullet weight and velocity a Type 1 bulletproof vest must stop. The skin stopped bullets fired at reduced speed but not at full speed. It was made with the Forensic Genomics Consortium Netherlands, Utah State University, Leiden University Medical Center and the Netherlands Forensic Institute.

## In their words

> You need to keep in mind that I didn’t start this project to create a skin capable of stopping a full magazine of AK-74 rounds. I did not aim for something "useful", instead I wanted to explorer how we deal with this fear of being penetrated.
>
> — Jalila Essaïdi, ['A Spider Under the Skin: Jalila Essaïdi’s Bulletproof Skin', interview, Irenebrination, 6 March 2013](https://www.irenebrination.com/2013/03/06/jalila-essaidi-bulletproof-skin/)

> I set out to create a bulletproof skin that would still be vulnerable, in other words, I was skeptical myself from the start and pleasantly surprised by the true potential of the material in the end.
>
> — Jalila Essaïdi, ['A Spider Under the Skin: Jalila Essaïdi’s Bulletproof Skin', interview, Irenebrination, 6 March 2013](https://www.irenebrination.com/2013/03/06/jalila-essaidi-bulletproof-skin/)

> Some interesting points are the ethical dilemma of the use of genetically modified organisms and donor material for artistic purposes and the ethical dilemma of the use of (artistic) research for unintended purposes.

> — Jalila Essaïdi, ['A Spider Under the Skin: Jalila Essaïdi’s Bulletproof Skin', interview, Irenebrination, 6 March 2013](https://www.irenebrination.com/2013/03/06/jalila-essaidi-bulletproof-skin/)

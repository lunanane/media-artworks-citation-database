# Circles of Rhythm and Rains

**Dinoj Mahendranathan**, 2025 · Sound / Robotics / kinetics

Circles of Rhythm and Rains (also given as Circle of Rhythms / Circle of Rhythms & Rains) is a kinetic sound installation shown at the Rio Complex for Colomboscope 2026, co-commissioned with 421 Arts Campus. Traditional Tamil drums used in Koothu (thavil, mathalam, udukkai, parai, mirudhangam), handcrafted with artisan Vadivel Aananthashankar from reclaimed wood and repurposed materials, are arranged in a circle and struck by automated tappers driven by Arduino and motors, built with DreamSpace Academy/lab. Visitors meet programmed, pulsating beat cycles played without a human drummer.

## In their words

> While machines can replicate a rhythm with mathematical precision, the installation highlights what is lost in the absence of a human performer. It serves as a critique: without the warmth of a palm, the nuance of finger pressure, and the soul of a living artist, the music remains mechanical and hollow.
>
> — Dinoj Mahendranathan, [Circle of Rhythms: An Interactive Sound Installation, project book ('Created by: Dinoj Mahendranathan'), linked from the artist's website](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1e2SClZ-Lkg8fXgXgZ6tIOts_oaugoZV3/view?usp=sharing)

<sub>says</sub>

> As interest among the younger generation wanes, this project imagines a future where these instruments exist without human hands to play them.
>
> — Dinoj Mahendranathan, [Circle of Rhythms: An Interactive Sound Installation, project book ('Created by: Dinoj Mahendranathan'), linked from the artist's website](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1e2SClZ-Lkg8fXgXgZ6tIOts_oaugoZV3/view?usp=sharing)

<sub>says</sub>

> Dissent appears through the mechanical rhythms and the tension they create. The installation plays with the contrast between human rhythm and machine-driven rhythm, reflecting how our cultural and environmental rhythms are disrupted by modern life. The automated beat becomes a subtle form of questioning: how do we hold onto heritage in a rapidly changing world?
>
> — Dinoj Mahendranathan, [The Art of Listening: In Conversation with Dinoj M, ARTRA Magazine](https://www.artra.lk/the-art-of-listening-in-conversation-with-dinoj-m/)

<sub>asks</sub>

> Renewal is at the core of the work. The installation continuously shifts, rhythms build, fade, restart, and reinterlock. This cyclical movement parallels how cultural practices evolve and regenerate with time. I believe this work will let the audience feel rhythm as a living thing; it remembers, it contests, and it changes.
>
> — Dinoj Mahendranathan, [The Art of Listening: In Conversation with Dinoj M, ARTRA Magazine](https://www.artra.lk/the-art-of-listening-in-conversation-with-dinoj-m/)

<sub>says</sub>

> It's also about archiving, especially endangered bird sounds, because sound is one of the first things we lose when ecosystems collapse.
>
> — Dinoj Mahendranathan, [The Art of Listening: In Conversation with Dinoj M, ARTRA Magazine](https://www.artra.lk/the-art-of-listening-in-conversation-with-dinoj-m/)

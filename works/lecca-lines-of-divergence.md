# Lines of Divergence

**Ximena Garrido-Lecca**, 2018 · Screen / monitor

Lines of Divergence is a 7'35" video documenting a current land occupation near Pucusana, a fishing village on the outskirts of Lima, where chalk lines drawn in the desert divide newly registered land into plots. It is the last chapter of the artist's documentation, begun in 2010, of the bamboo-and-wood structures built in the desert to claim land, a process often called 'invasions' that has shaped Peru's coast since the 1950s. Her 2018 solo exhibition at Galerie Gisela Capitain, Cologne, was titled Lines of Divergence, and the video was shown at the 21st Bienal Sesc\_Videobrasil (2019).

## In their words

> Being an accessible material, it is often used to demark territory in land invasions, thus symbolizing a gesture of resistance against government neglect, particularly of indigenous and poor communities, and ongoing processes of cultural erasure that come with globalization.
>
> — Ximena Garrido-Lecca, [Statement 2019 (transcription of the statement for the 21st Biennial), Videobrasil archive](https://site.videobrasil.org.br/en/acervo/artistas/textos/1781527)

> When it comes to the tension between public and private space in Peru I have explored, through some works, ideas around the privatisation of the landscape and the co-option of natural resources, reviewing the social impacts and bad distribution of resources. I am interested in tracing peoples’ adaptability around these conditions and how new forms of resistance arises from them.
>
> — Ximena Garrido-Lecca, [Statement 2019 (transcription of the statement for the 21st Biennial), Videobrasil archive](https://site.videobrasil.org.br/en/acervo/artistas/textos/1781527)

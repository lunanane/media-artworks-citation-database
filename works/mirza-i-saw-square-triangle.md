# I Saw Square Triangle Sine

**Haroon Mirza**, 2011 · Sound / Light / Material / sculpture

An installation made for Camden Arts Centre (7 October 2011 – 8 January 2012) that brings together band instruments – a keyboard, drum kit and synthesisers – with turntables, LED lighting, lamps and radios to produce a minimal audio composition. It includes paintings from Angus Fairhurst's Underdone / Overdone Paintings (1998) with a drum kit, and visitors can play the drums, adding their own noise or rhythm to the installation. The work toured to Spike Island, Bristol, in 2012.

## In their words

> Sometimes in an installation, I would include an existing artwork by another artist, either by loan, purchase or simply by working with another artist collaboratively. In the end, it’s about material. I have to break it down and treat it as a material form like a cable or speaker or television, whatever.
>
> — Haroon Mirza, [Interview with Selina Ting, InitiArt Magazine (2013), archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20190906083637/http://www.initiartmagazine.com/interview.php?IVarchive=105)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> The idea is that any noise is a sound. Any sound can be music if you listen to it in that way. If someone who doesn’t know my work, s/he would probably find a lot of noises when they encounter my work. At some point, s/he might find it quite musical.
>
> — Haroon Mirza, [Interview with Selina Ting, InitiArt Magazine (2013), archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20190906083637/http://www.initiartmagazine.com/interview.php?IVarchive=105)

<sub>says · about their practice</sub>

> The whole concept is to question the importance of visual culture but still try to operate within visual space.
>
> — Haroon Mirza, [Interview with Selina Ting, InitiArt Magazine (2013), archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20190906083637/http://www.initiartmagazine.com/interview.php?IVarchive=105)

> So I often work with works of art and incorporate them into my own installations – it’s turning works of art into material for art.
>
> — Haroon Mirza, [Jon Cronshaw, 'Art: Forms and functions - an interview with Haroon Mirza' (Yorkshire Post, 17 May 2013), archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20140507090146/http://www.joncronshaw.co.uk/2013/05/art-forms-and-functions-interview-with.html)

# Between the Waves

**Tejal Shah**, 2012 · Screen / monitor / Sound / Performance / body

Between the Waves is a five-channel video installation with multi-channel sound (total running time 86 minutes) in which performers wearing unicorn-like horns and costumes made from refuse appear on a polluted shore, in a mangrove forest, under water and on a landfill, alongside animation and a burning moon. Channel 1 is a longer film that forms the backbone of the installation; the channel Landfill Dance, made with dancers of the Hrishikesh Pawar Contemporary Dance Company and Linda Borini, serves as an epilogue. It was commissioned by Carolyn Christov-Bakargiev and premiered at dOCUMENTA (13) in Kassel in 2012.

## In their words

> Channel 1 is a circular fable, a new cosmology bearing a strong commentary on the Anthropocene. It’s a creation myth about a forgotten civilization whose existence and actions precipitate certain relationships between more-than-humans and humans.
>
> — Tejal Shah, [Nanda van den Berg, An Interview with Tejal Shah on Landfill Dance, part of Between the Waves, Huis Marseille](https://huismarseille.nl/interview-tejal-shah/)

<sub>says</sub>

> Landfill Dance functions as an epilogue in the installation. Set into the future of the long past, it imagines a possible scope of what remains.
>
> — Tejal Shah, [Nanda van den Berg, An Interview with Tejal Shah on Landfill Dance, part of Between the Waves, Huis Marseille](https://huismarseille.nl/interview-tejal-shah/)

<sub>says</sub>

> Finally, I decided to work with contemporary dance in this project to create seemingly nonsensical movements within the setting of a landfill to think about the futility of our gestures of sense making and recuperation in the Anthropocene (with a sense of humour of course!).
>
> — Tejal Shah, [Nanda van den Berg, An Interview with Tejal Shah on Landfill Dance, part of Between the Waves, Huis Marseille](https://huismarseille.nl/interview-tejal-shah/)

<sub>says</sub>

> The landfill is one kind of an archeological site of the future, they are an accumulation of our refuse that are sometimes transformed by structures built on top of them – like the plan to build an amusement park on top of the landfill in my video.
>
> — Tejal Shah, [Nanda van den Berg, An Interview with Tejal Shah on Landfill Dance, part of Between the Waves, Huis Marseille](https://huismarseille.nl/interview-tejal-shah/)

<sub>says</sub>

> So the material takes on oracular qualities, there is a feedback process and I am co-creating the work with the work and its materials themselves.
>
> — Tejal Shah, [Nanda van den Berg, An Interview with Tejal Shah on Landfill Dance, part of Between the Waves, Huis Marseille](https://huismarseille.nl/interview-tejal-shah/)

# Dust Blooms: A Research Narrative in Artistic Ecology

**Alexandra Regan Toland**, 2016 · Material / sculpture / Sensor / interaction / Text / print

Dust Blooms examines how well urban flowers filter airborne dust, in three parts. Dust was collected from seven wild roadside plant species on heavily trafficked Berlin streets and analysed under light microscopes; an exhibition laboratory with microscopes, slides, protocols and maps lets visitors look at the dust-covered petals themselves, next to a raised bed of the same species and photographs. It also includes seven botanical engravings printed with the collected street dust and shell gold, and dandelion-based sculptural prototypes made from consumer materials, with Arduino dust sensors that measure atmospheric dust levels.

> Dust Blooms investigates the ability of flowers to not only provide beauty but to help filter atmospheric particulate matter (PM), otherwise known as dust, from natural sources such as pollen and anthropogenic sources such as industrial and vehicular emissions and tire abrasion.

> — Alexandra Regan Toland (artist's website), ['Dust Blooms at Museum Schloß Moyland', artoland.com (2016)](https://artoland.com/2016/06/28/dust-blooms/)

# Octopus

**Yoshua Okón**, 2011 · Projection / Performance / body

Octopus is a multichannel video installation (four synchronized projections, about 18:30 minutes, shown with Home Depot buckets) produced during the artist's residency at the Hammer Museum, Los Angeles. It shows a dozen Guatemalan Mayan immigrants, day laborers who fought in the Guatemalan civil war, some on both sides, reenacting that war in the parking lot of a Home Depot store in Los Angeles where they wait to be hired. The title refers to the Guatemalan nickname for the United Fruit Company.

## In their words

> My original motivation to make this piece was the huge ignorance there is with regards to U.S. foreign policy and to root causes for immigration,
>
> — Yoshua Okón, [Logan Saether, 'An 'Octopus' is being used at ASU's Project Space to start discussion on Guatemalan immigration', The State Press, September 2015](https://www.statepress.com/article/2015/09/yoshua-okon-octopus-coming-to-asu)

>
> — Yoshua Okón, [Logan Saether, 'An 'Octopus' is being used at ASU's Project Space to start discussion on Guatemalan immigration', The State Press, September 2015](https://www.statepress.com/article/2015/09/yoshua-okon-octopus-coming-to-asu)
